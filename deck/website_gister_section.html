Gister: drafting by predicting latent futures

Gister is our own drafter, not a modification of DFlash or EAGLE. Stage 1 compresses the target’s hidden states for 8 positions (layers 18, 24, 30 and the final layer) into one gist latent and decodes it back to exact tokens through the target’s frozen output layer. Stage 2 is a search network that predicts the next gist latent from the current gist and the target’s raw states. Decoding that predicted future gives 8 draft tokens, which the target verifies in one pass, so the output stays the target’s.

Gister’s chain costs the same per round as DFlash; DFlash wins on accepted tokens. Sampled trees raise τ 18 to 32% but cost 45 to 55% more per round here (sampling the latents plus verifying 60 to 120 nodes with 4-bit kernels), so on this hardware every tree is slower than the chain. An earlier “+12% tree speedup on GSM8K” came from a run where desktop apps throttled the GPU and is withdrawn. Drafters with the same verification width give identical text; wider verification diverges at near-ties under 4-bit numerics. All results are greedy decoding only.

What moved stage 2: giving it the target’s raw states (+29% math). What did not: 64 positions of history, a 7x bigger network on the same 2,000 answers (it memorised them), or dropping the latent loss. The 154M overfit is why v2 trains on far more on-policy data: Qwen3-8B’s own non-thinking answers from PerfectBlend plus our own, about 100x more text, computed live so nothing is cached to disk.
