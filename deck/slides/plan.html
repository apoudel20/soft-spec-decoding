09 · THE PLAN AND THE ASK

Make the latent a plan, then test whether a plan pays

Today’s latent is close to a token codebook, because an 8-token reconstruction gate rewards memorizing tokens. The compute would fund the version where the latent has to plan.

Gister as a planner

· A plan latent over the next 64 to 256 tokens, too long to memorize.

· Learned JEPA-style from the target’s future states, with no token loss on the plan.

· A flow sampler predicts the next plan, so tree branches differ in intent, not spelling.

· A realizer trained around the plan writes the next 8 to 16 tokens. The target verifies them, so output is unchanged.

1 · Oracle plan

Realizer with the true future plan vs without. A large lift funds the rest. No lift is itself a result about drafting.

2 · Predicted plan

Swap the oracle for the sampler and measure how much of the lift survives.

3 · Plans as trees

Branches from different plans vs DFlash / DDTree per-slot trees at the same node count.

4 · Scale

100M+ on-policy positions and a 0.5B to 1B sampler. Here, 800M positions is about 3 days of target passes.

Why earlier plan tests don’t settle it: each added one averaged future vector to a frozen DFlash trained without plans, with 300 answers of tuning. Today’s full-context test (56 earlier positions) moved best of 8 from 3.72 / 2.64 / 1.72 to 4.01 / 2.66 / 1.75, so the predictor’s inputs are not the main limit. Risks: the oracle may not help even when built in, and JEPA latents can collapse.

The ask is for the version of the idea that is actually new: a latent that plans over a long horizon, predicted in latent space, with branches that are different plans. The first experiment is decisive either way and takes days, not months.
