9 · PROPOSED WORK

A longer-horizon latent

The current latent is trained to reconstruct 8 tokens exactly, and it mostly encodes those tokens. We propose a latent that summarizes a longer future and is trained by prediction in latent space.

Approach

· An encoder maps the target’s hidden states over the next 64 to 256 tokens to z.

· Encoder and predictor are trained JEPA-style (EMA target encoder, variance regularization), with no token reconstruction loss.

· Stage 2 samples z with flow matching. Each tree branch comes from a different sample.

· A decoder conditioned on z and the context drafts the next 8 to 16 tokens. The target verifies them.

1 · Oracle z

Train the encoder first and freeze it. Train two decoders from scratch, one given z of the true future, one given z = 0. Continue only if the first accepts at least 20% more tokens on code and chat.

2 · Predicted z

Replace the oracle z with stage-2 samples and measure how much of the gain remains.

3 · Trees

Compare with DFlash and DDTree per-position trees at equal verified nodes.

4 · Scale

100M+ positions of Qwen3-8B outputs and a 0.5B to 1B stage 2. States are computed on the fly, since caching 4 layers for 100M positions takes about 3.2 TB.

Compute estimate for test 1: about one week on 4 A100- or H100-class GPUs, including data generation. Tests 2 to 4 depend on its result. Earlier experiments (RESULTS.md) added one averaged future vector to DFlash, frozen and fine-tuned with LoRA on 300 answers, and saw no gain even with the true future. They do not test a decoder trained from scratch with a latent of the future. Risks: the oracle may still not help, and latent prediction objectives can collapse without regularization.

Test 1 determines whether tests 2 to 4 are worth running.
