# Condition the drafter on target features, not soft tokens

The 2023 to 2026 literature supports one of the three hypotheses in a stronger form and argues against the other two. **H3 should stay**, but "one self-conditioning pass" should become full-block training-time test on the drafter's own rollouts, with target soft labels computed on-policy and the KL vs TV choice demoted to an ablation. **H1 should be replaced.** The verified way for a target to help draft its own future is a separate few-layer drafter that reads multi-layer target hidden states and reuses verifier states (DFlash, DFlow, ConFu, SD²), not gated LoRA on extra target positions. The Apple gated-LoRA method averages 3.17 tokens per step and reports no wall-clock numbers, while DFlash reaches τ 6.49 and 4.86x on Qwen3-8B. **H2 should be dropped as stated.** Every matched drafting test of soft token inputs found was neutral or negative, and the one positive second-pass result conditions on a continuous feature rather than on E^T q. Interpretability work says hidden states carry token content only about 8 tokens ahead, topic about one paragraph ahead, and only coarse scalars (length, step count, answer, correctness) for the whole response, with weak or null planning evidence in Qwen models below 4B. The latent-plan idea survives only as a delta on ConFu, SD² and Future Summary Prediction, and its biggest risk is that a plan head reading the same frozen features a DFlash drafter already sees adds no new information. The first experiment should therefore measure how much more the 1.5B target knows about the future than the 0.5B drafter before anything is built.

Throughout, τ is mean accepted tokens per target forward pass and "x" is wall-clock speedup over autoregressive decoding unless marked tokens per step. No paper found reports results on a Qwen2.5-1.5B target, so every absolute number below is a transfer estimate.

## Two of three hypotheses need replacing

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Replacement | Strongest evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1, target refines its own future guesses via gated LoRA on extra positions | Replace | Few-layer block drafter with multi-layer target features injected into K/V (DFlash), plus verifier-state reuse at rejected positions (DFlow), plus a sampler head for within-block dependence | DFlash τ 6.49 and 4.86x on Qwen3-8B vs Apple 3.17 tokens per step averaged over 13 tasks |
| H2, soft distributions E^T q as inputs beat hard tokens | Drop | H2', a second pass conditioned on continuous pass-1 state, emitted as extra tree candidates | Soft inputs never beat hard by more than about 1% τ in drafting tests. Feature-conditioned second pass gives +6.3% to +10.2% τ |
| H3, self-conditioned drafter trained on target distributions | Keep and strengthen | Full-block training-time test, on-policy KD, divergence as a tuned ablation, exponential position weights | EAGLE-3 acceptance stays nearly flat with offset. DistillSpec finds no universal divergence winner |

## Training-time test closes the offset collapse that one pass only halves

Our pattern of flat teacher-forced accuracy (about 0.91 GSM8K, 0.77 MT-Bench, 0.95 HumanEval) with free-running acceptance collapsing by block offset is the exposure-bias signature that the strongest autoregressive drafters were redesigned to fix. EAGLE-3 trains the drafter on its own sampled predictions with attention masks that mimic the inference-time tree, and its Figure 7 shows **EAGLE-1 acceptance dropping significantly as self-generated inputs accumulate while EAGLE-3 stays almost unchanged** ([EAGLE-3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840)). On LLaMA-3.1-8B, removing the feature-regression constraint that blocks this training raised **MT-Bench τ from 4.05 to 5.37 and GSM8K τ from 4.24 to 5.22**, and adding fused low, mid and high target features pushed τ to 6.13 and 6.23 ([EAGLE-3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840)). The paper does not separate training-time test from the loss change, so the +1.2 τ is an upper bound on what self-conditioning alone buys. HASS attacks the same train and decode mismatch with "harmonized context alignment" and reports 8% to 20% over EAGLE-2 ([HASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15766)), and Falcon's glancing distillation, which exposes the drafter to partially correct blocks, lifts τ from 2.78 to 3.34 on Vicuna-7B MT-Bench ([Falcon](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.12639)). Our "one pass recovers about half the gap" fits this literature well. EAGLE-3 unrolls the whole draft depth during training, and we unroll one step.

The soft-label half of H3 also holds, but its emphasis is wrong. DistillSpec proved that token-level acceptance equals 1 minus TVD between target and drafter, yet found that **directly optimizing TVD "was not best in most settings"** ([DistillSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08461)). Its best divergence changed with task and decoding mode, with JSD on XSum greedy, forward KL on GSM8K greedy with draft-generated data, and reverse KL on GSM8K at T=1 with target-generated data. What mattered more was the data. Model-generated sequences beat fixed ground truth, draft-generated data matched target-generated data at lower cost, and SeqKD with one-hot labels did poorly, so white-box target logits are worth keeping ([DistillSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08461)). Distillation gave 10% to 45% over an undistilled draft there, which sets the realistic size of this lever. DFlash, the strongest block drafter, uses plain cross-entropy on about 800K target-regenerated responses with **position weights w_k = exp(-(k-1)/γ), γ = 7 for block 16**, because acceptance is a prefix product and early positions dominate τ ([DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1)).

The keep verdict therefore comes with three changes. Unroll self-conditioning across the full block instead of one pass. Compute target soft labels on the drafter's own rollouts. Report FKL, RKL and TVD at both T=0 and T=1 as an ablation rather than a headline. Our task spread (MT-Bench 0.77 vs HumanEval 0.95) mirrors PARD's finding that even fully independent mask predictions keep fourth-position acceptance at 0.85 to 0.90 on code and math ([PARD](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18583)), so chat is where the coherence gap lives and where improvements should be reported.

## Target-fed block drafters, not gated LoRA, lead the field

H1 is a near reproduction of Samragh et al. at Apple, which fine-tunes Tulu3-8B with gated LoRA (rank 128) on 8 mask positions so the base model's outputs stay unchanged. It averages **3.17 tokens per step across 13 tasks**, rising to 5.22 on GSM8K and 5.35 on HumanEval but only 2.52 on AlpacaEval and 1.91 on PopQA ([Apple 2507.11851](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11851)). These are tokens per step, not wall-clock, verification is greedy token matching, and the paper does not say whether sampling is supported. Ranks above 128 reduced speedup. The method's transferable parts are its sampler head, a 2-layer MLP that conditions each position on the previously sampled token, and quadratic decoding, which beats linear decoding by a margin that grows with mask count ([Apple 2507.11851](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11851)).

DFlash shows what the same goal looks like at the frontier. A 5-layer block drafter takes hidden states from 5 target layers, projects them, and injects them into the K and V of every drafter layer, with bidirectional attention inside a block of 16 and a frozen shared embedding and LM head. On Qwen3-8B it reports **τ 6.49 and 4.86x at T=0 and τ 5.48 and 4.03x at T=1, against EAGLE-3 at τ 3.40 and 2.02x in the same harness** ([DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1)). The comparison with Apple is not controlled, since models and tasks differ, and paper-reported speedups shrink under third-party harnesses. Spec-Bench measured EAGLE-3 at 3.02x on Vicuna-13B where the EAGLE-3 paper reported 5.51x ([Spec-Bench](https://github.com/hemingkx/Spec-Bench/blob/main/Leaderboard.md), [EAGLE-3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840)). Even with that discount, the gap is large enough to move the project.

The lossless version of "the target refines its guesses during verification" already exists as DFlow. It reuses the verifier's hidden states at rejected positions to guide the next drafting round at no extra target compute and trains with a "self-condition" strategy that feeds verifier representations from earlier predictions into later ones, reporting consistently longer acceptance than DFlash ([DFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06498)). Its exact margin was not in the abstract. ConFu and SD² feed target-derived future vectors to the drafter with frozen target weights (covered below). All three keep losslessness trivially because the token-path computation never changes. ConFu states the constraint directly, noting that speculative decoding "does not permit fine-tuning the target model" ([ConFu](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)), and Medusa-2's joint fine-tuning is the cautionary case that the EAGLE-3 authors exclude from T=1 results as lossy ([EAGLE-3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840)).

Drafter scale pushes in the same direction. Our 0.5B drafter is a third of the 1.5B target. On Qwen2.5-3B, PARD measured a vanilla small autoregressive drafter at **1.55x against 2.65x for a one-pass parallel drafter**, and the gap stayed large at 7B (1.97x vs 3.18x) ([PARD](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18583)). Cross-position conditioning inside a block is the other cheap win. Medusa to Hydra raised Spec-Bench τ from 2.39 to 3.35 on Vicuna-13B ([Spec-Bench](https://github.com/hemingkx/Spec-Bench/blob/main/Leaderboard.md)), and Hydra reports up to 1.31x over Medusa ([Hydra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05109)). The replacement for H1 is a DFlash-style few-layer drafter reading Qwen2.5-1.5B features, with DFlow-style verifier reuse and an Apple-style sampler head. If gated LoRA is kept at all, it needs wall-clock numbers, a sampling-mode exactness argument and a bit-identical logit test to be more than a reproduction.

## Soft token inputs lost every matched drafting test found

No paper was found in which feeding E^T q or any token distribution into a drafter raised acceptance. The most direct evidence is the open EmbeddingSuperPosition study on Qwen3-4B GSM8K, which tested soft priors in DFlash, EAGLE-3 and gated-LoRA self-speculation ([EmbeddingSuperPosition](https://github.com/bijmuj/EmbeddingSuperPosition)). Training-free, a two-pass soft input on DFlash cut τ from 3.073 to 2.763 and a hidden-state variant cut it to 2.524. After fine-tuning, a soft two-pass DFlash drafter scored **3.769 against 3.832 for a compute-matched control while doubling draft time from 6.6 ms to 13.1 ms**. EAGLE-3 with supertoken feedback reached τ 4.86 against 5.42 for hard true-token feedback. Gated-LoRA self-speculation on Qwen3-1.7B moved τ by a factor of 1.012 and throughput by 0.998. Under sampling the soft-trained drafter fell to 0.91 of the hard-trained τ at T=0.6 and **0.85 at T=1.0**, exactly where distributions are most multimodal ([EmbeddingSuperPosition](https://github.com/bijmuj/EmbeddingSuperPosition)).

The reasoning literature explains why. Wu et al. found that the forward pass prunes a soft input to its top-1 component, with the JS divergence between Soft Thinking's output and the top-1-only output concentrated around 0, so vanilla Soft Thinking behaves like greedy decoding (71.57 vs 72.13 average on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03440v4)). Pure distribution input, the "Direct Mixture" arm of Mixture of Inputs, lost 6 to 12 average points on 3 of 4 models and **dropped LiveCodeBench from 76.32 to 53.42 on QwQ-32B** ([Mixture of Inputs](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14827v3)). Soft inputs helped only with a hard anchor (MoI, +1.3 to +2.4 points), with reinjected noise (Gumbel or Dirichlet), or with training to consume them, and the gains were in reasoning accuracy, not drafting ([Mixture of Inputs](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14827v3), [Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03440v4), [Soft Tokens, Hard Truths](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19170)).

The positive drafting result points elsewhere. Parallel Reconsideration gives an EAGLE-3.1 drafter a gated second pass on uncertain positions, conditioned on the first-pass feature and a learned self-query, and adds the resampled token as an extra tree candidate. It reports **+6.3% to +10.2% τ** across Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B and Gemma-3-12B, including T=1 ([Parallel Reconsideration](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05724v1)). Feeding the committed hard token embedding instead cut τ by 4.1%, and spending the same compute on a second decoder layer gave only +2.0% τ with lower speedup. Diffusion LMs show the same thing at larger scale. Trained self-conditioning cut OWT generative perplexity from 42.89 to 23.72 in SCMDM ([SCMDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26985)), and Loopholing names the "sampling wall" where one-hot sampling discards information later steps need ([Loopholing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19304)). Both compare self-conditioning against none, not soft against hard, and Loopholing's fix carries a hidden state rather than a token-embedding mixture.

H2 should be dropped and replaced by H2', which says our two-pass gain comes from conditioning on continuous pass-1 state plus extra compute, not from the softness of token inputs. Uncertainty is better expressed by branching (extra tree candidates) than by blending into one input vector, which is how every positive drafting result handles it. The EmbeddingSuperPosition oracle that fused correct priors at the output side gained 4.8 τ while the input-side oracle lost 1.3 ([EmbeddingSuperPosition](https://github.com/bijmuj/EmbeddingSuperPosition)). That makes logit-level fusion with stale target logits the only soft channel worth a secondary probe, and the oracle number is an upper bound, not an estimate.

## Hidden states encode about 8 tokens of content and a few scalars beyond

How far ahead a target "knows" its future sets the ceiling for any plan the target emits. The answer depends sharply on what is decoded.

| Horizon | What is decodable | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 tokens | Specific tokens, 44% to 48% precision@1 with a learned decoder, 16% to 29% with a linear probe (GPT-J-6B) | [Future Lens](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.04897) |
| About 8 tokens | Linear probe on general text overlaps the unigram baseline at every layer (Qwen3, Gemma-3, Llama-3) | [Where's the Plan](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07984) |
| One paragraph | Topic-level semantic embedding, cosine 0.55 vs 0.50 for 5 true tokens, 76% subject match but 3% at moderate detail (Llama-3.2-3B) | [ParaScopes](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00180v1) |
| Whole response | Length, step count, chosen answer, story character, correctness | [Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06258), [Merzouk et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05316), [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05419v1) |

Token-level look-ahead is short and concentrated where the model is already confident. Future Lens accuracy at N=1 is 26% when model confidence is below 30% and 95% when it is above 90% ([Future Lens](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.04897)), so the decodable future tokens are largely the ones a drafter already gets right. ParaScopes, reading the residual stream at "\n\n", summarizes its own result as a lookahead of 5 to 10 tokens ([ParaScopes](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00180v1)). Whole-response properties are decodable from the prompt's last hidden state, but Dong et al.'s MLP probes saturate at about 16 hidden units for structure and 32 for content ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06258)). Those plans are low-dimensional.

Scale matters, and our models sit below the documented thresholds. Wu et al. measured only a **0.12-nat myopia gap** on a 124M model, meaning deliberate future-directed computation barely helps loss at that size, though the gap grows across Pythia 70M to 2.8B ([Wu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00859v2)). In rhyme planning, Claude 3.5 Haiku holds a planned line-ending word at the preceding newline and injecting that feature steers the ending 70% of the time ([Biology of an LLM](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)). Open-model replications disagree on how small this goes. Ma and Rui find the newline probe gap's confidence interval includes zero for Qwen3 0.6B to 8B, a causal newline site only in Gemma-3-27B, and last-word causal planning in Qwen only from 4B ([Where's the Plan](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07984)). Maar et al. call implicit planning universal from 1B but "weaker in smaller models", with clean newline steering only in Gemma2-9B and Gemma3-27B ([Maar et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20164)). Dong et al. include Qwen-2.5-Instruct 1.5B and find structure planning that strengthens with size within a family ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06258)). Ma and Rui add the crucial caveat that **probe signal and causal use are dissociable**.

For the latent-plan idea this has three consequences. A plan spanning 32 tokens to the whole response can carry topic and structure, not token identities, so its benefit to drafting must come through coarse conditioning such as step boundaries, answer commitment and remaining length. The quantity that matters is the target's future information minus what the drafter computes from the same prefix, and no paper measures it. Finally, and this is our inference rather than a published finding, a plan head reading the same frozen hidden states that a DFlash drafter already sees through K/V injection cannot add information. It can only add a better training signal for extracting it, or extra target compute, which is what ConFu buys with its contemplate tokens. Correctness and length probes, which work even on R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B with ECE of 0.04 on MATH ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05419v1)), are better suited to adaptive draft length than to telling the drafter what to write.

## ConFu already owns the target-emits-a-vector claim

ConFu is the closest prior art. The target stays frozen, learnable soft-prompt tokens are prepended to its KV cache, and an appended contemplate token's last-layer hidden state becomes a future vector that conditions an EAGLE-3 drafter. An attention mask lets only contemplate tokens see the soft prompts, so ordinary prefix representations are unchanged and the method is lossless ([ConFu](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)). The vector is trained only through the drafter's KL loss under train-time testing with "no additional auxiliary losses", over a rollout of about 7 tokens, and verification processes 2T tokens for T draft nodes. On SpecBench at T=0 it lifts **Llama-3.1-8B from τ 4.59 to 5.01 and speedup from 2.36x to 2.69x**, and Qwen3-4B by about 21% τ ([ConFu](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)). SD² builds a steering vector from verifier states at layers 3, L/2 and L-2, adds it as a bias to every drafter MLP once per drafting stage, and trains it with a random offset in [1, k] so it must encode the next k tokens. It reports up to 35% more accepted tokens under sampling and an average speedup gain of 16.3% at T=1 ([SD²](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09844)). Future Summary Prediction trains an auxiliary head on the target to regress a reverse LM's summary of the long-term future, gaining up to 5% on math and coding at 3B and 8B, but never uses it for acceleration ([FSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14751)).

| Claim for the latent-plan idea | Survives? | Closest prior art |
|---|---|---|
| First to have the target emit a latent that conditions a lossless drafter | No | [ConFu](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899), [SD²](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09844) |
| First lossless drafter conditioned on target internals | No | [EAGLE-3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840), [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1) |
| Gated LoRA that preserves target outputs | No | [Apple 2507.11851](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11851) |
| Target refines future guesses from verification-pass states | No | [DFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06498) |
| Name "hierarchical speculative decoding" | No | Taken by a lossless verification scheme ([HSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05724)) |
| Plan horizon far beyond the draft block (32 tokens to whole response), refreshed as tokens are accepted | Yes | ConFu has no defined horizon, SD² covers k tokens |
| Explicit representation objective for the plan (FSP reverse-LM summary, VAE or JEPA) plus acceptance loss, shown to beat KL-only vectors | Yes, if the ablation wins | [FSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14751), [Semformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11143), [LLM-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14252) supply objectives but no drafting results |
| Two-level drafter with a plan head and a token head | Yes | ConFu uses one EAGLE-3 layer with one extra input. [DBLast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05448)'s latent is drafter-internal |
| Zero-extra-token plan side head that avoids ConFu's 2T verification cost | Yes | ConFu |
| Plan conditioning applied to a one-pass block drafter at far offsets | Yes | ConFu and SD² use autoregressive drafters |

Every step-level or hierarchical speculation method found relaxes acceptance to semantic equivalence or reward and is lossy, including SpecReason, Lookahead Reasoning, Reward-Guided SD and SemanticSpec ([SpecReason](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07891v2), [Lookahead Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19830v1), [RSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19324), [SemanticSpec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03708)). The clean framing is therefore that the plan improves drafts while token verification stays exact, which none of them does. Two cautions apply. Web indexing of 2026 arXiv is patchy and no citation chase was run on papers citing ConFu or FSP, so a closer paper could exist. And ConFu's measured gain of roughly 8% to 21% τ is the realistic prior for what a target-side future signal is worth, not the large step change a long horizon suggests. Losslessness holds relative to the original target only if the plan path leaves token-path logits bit-identical, which ConFu guarantees through frozen weights and mask isolation and which a gated-LoRA variant must test explicitly.

## Conclusion

The three hypotheses and the latent-plan idea converge on one mechanism, which is moving continuous target state into the drafter and training the drafter on its own rollouts. Our coherence gap is most likely an information gap before it is a within-block dependence gap. A standalone 0.5B drafter conditions only on tokens, and every method that closed a similar gap (EAGLE-3, DFlash, DFlow, ConFu, SD²) did so by feeding target features. If a DFlash-style drafter reading Qwen2.5-1.5B features flattens our per-offset acceptance curve on its own, the remaining room for H1, H2 or a plan is small, and the paper should be framed around that measurement.

The latent plan is best reframed as a question with a measurable answer, namely what the 1.5B target knows about the next 32 to 256 tokens that its own features do not already hand to the drafter. Interpretability says the honest answer at this scale is topic and structure, not tokens, so the plan's most plausible payoff is in chat and long-form responses, where our teacher-forced accuracy is lowest (0.77 on MT-Bench) and where adaptive block length driven by length or step-boundary scalars has room to help. A ConFu-sized gain with zero extra verification tokens and an explicit long-horizon objective would be a publishable delta. A claim of a new paradigm would not survive review.

## Next experiments for one 12 GB GPU

Qwen2.5-1.5B in bf16 takes about 3 GB and stays frozen in every experiment below, so each fits on one 12 GB card with a few-layer drafter, gradient checkpointing and modest sequence lengths. Report τ, per-offset conditional acceptance and tokens per second at T=0 and T=1 on GSM8K, HumanEval and MT-Bench throughout.

1. **Measure the target-minus-drafter future gap.** Fit linear and 1-hidden-layer MLP probes on frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-0.5B hidden states for the model's own token at k = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, for a paragraph embedding and for remaining length. If the 1.5B advantage is near zero beyond k = 8, cut the long-horizon token claims and keep only structural plan targets.
2. **Replace the standalone 0.5B drafter with a DFlash-style drafter.** Build a 2 to 5 layer block drafter (block 8 to 16) with multi-layer Qwen2.5-1.5B features injected into K/V, the target's frozen embedding and LM head, and exp(-(k-1)/γ) position weights, trained on target-regenerated responses. Compare its per-offset acceptance curve against the vanilla 0.5B drafter and EAGLE-3, and add a causal vs bidirectional within-block ablation, which no paper has published.
3. **Unroll self-conditioning across the full block with on-policy KD.** On the drafter from step 2, compare 0, 1, 2 and full-block training-time test, with target soft labels on drafter rollouts and FKL, RKL and TVD as arms. This tests whether "recovers half the gap" becomes "recovers most of it".
4. **Run the H2' pass-2 input ablation at matched compute and data.** Arms are mask only, argmax embedding, top-k truncated E^T q, the MoI hard-anchor hybrid, and the pass-1 hidden state, with pass-2 tokens added as extra tree candidates. If mask only matches the rest, the two-pass gain is pure compute.
5. **Add a sampler head and a small tree.** Test an Apple-style 2-layer MLP that conditions each block position on the previous sampled token, then a tens-of-nodes tree, since verification of a 1.5B target at batch 1 is cheap.
6. **Test the plan against ConFu at equal cost.** On the step 2 drafter, compare a reproduced ConFu contemplate-token vector (KL only), a zero-extra-token plan head trained with an FSP-style reverse-LM summary loss at horizons 32, 128 and full response, and features alone. Include a bit-identical token-path logit check and a T=1 distribution test to confirm losslessness against the original target.
