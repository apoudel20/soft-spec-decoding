# Gist pruning trims drafter cost, rarely adds acceptance

The closest existing work is **ChainFlow (arXiv 2611.08669)**. It already does most of the proposed pipeline in a narrower form. A drafter of 0.53B to 1.64B parameters predicts the target's next 8 hidden states in a VAE-compressed space 4x to 5x narrower than the target. It then uses those predicted futures to pick a shared set of 8,192 candidate tokens before scoring drafts. That candidate cut bought **13% more throughput with acceptance length unchanged**, which is the best available estimate of what gist-driven pruning is worth. Separately, a large 2025 to 2026 literature on drafter vocabulary pruning (FR-Spec, DynaSpec, SpecVocab, NanoSpec, EvoSpec) shows that lossless candidate restriction recovers 94% to 98% of full-vocabulary acceptance. A training-free in-context set of under 3k tokens already matches learned routers. None of them conditions the cut on a predicted summary of the next 16 to hundreds of tokens, and no lossless drafter compresses many future tokens into one gist vector and then prunes a tree or vocabulary with it. That combination is open. The evidence says it is open mainly because it has little room to help. A hard mask built from information the drafter already has can only re-rank the drafter's own beliefs. Our own probes found the 1.5B target knows little more than the 0.5B drafter about its future gist (R² 0.37 vs 0.34). Shared geometry between same-family models is real but beside the point, since speculative decoding supplies unlimited paired data and Qwen models already share a vocabulary. The remaining idea is a modest efficiency technique, not an acceptance breakthrough. It can be tested offline in about an hour of GPU time, and the main kill criterion is whether a gist mask changes the drafter's top-1 agreement with the target by at least +1.5 points.

Throughout, τ is mean accepted tokens per target forward pass, "x" is wall-clock speedup over autoregressive decoding, and "lossless" means exact target verification that preserves the output distribution.

## ChainFlow already predicts the target's future and prunes with it

The idea has three parts. A drafter-side model forecasts a compact embedding of what the target will say. The forecast lives in a space both models share. The forecast then narrows the drafter's search before tokens are drafted. Each part exists somewhere, and ChainFlow covers the first and third together. It trains a transformer VAE over windows of up to 16 target final-layer states, **compressing 2560 dims to 640 for Qwen3.5-4B and 4096 or 5120 to 1024 for 9B and 27B**. A one-step flow-matching network then predicts K = 8 future latents from 8 context latents. The decoded states go through the target's frozen LM head, and "one matrix product of h^1:K against the shortlist selects a shared set of M=8192 candidate ids" ([ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)). On Qwen3.5-4B at batch 1 the 8x4 tree reaches **τ 2.78 and 1.87x, against DFlash at 1.69x and EAGLE-3 at 1.35x**, and 2.12x at 27B. Two-pass rescoring of the 8,192 candidates raises throughput from 540.4 to 611.7 tokens/s with τ about 2.78 either way ([ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)). The limits matter for anyone building on it. Its latent is one per token, compressed along channels rather than time, so it is not a multi-token gist. Tree verification is greedy-only, and the authors report that **4% to 13% of prompts produce outputs different from plain greedy decoding**, which they attribute to fp16 near-ties. Its speedup falls to **0.94x at 8 concurrent requests and 0.45x at 128** ([ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)).

Three points stand out. First, after EAGLE-3 the field stopped asking drafters to regress future target representations. Its authors found feature prediction was "an additional constraint, which limits the expressiveness of the draft model", and removing it lifted τ from 4.05 to 5.37 on LLaMA-3.1-8B MT-bench ([EAGLE-3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840v3)). ChainFlow is the exception. Its tree τ of 2.78 beats EAGLE-3's 2.49 and DFlash's 2.35 in the same table, but its chain mode reaches only τ 2.01 and 1.23x, so the edge comes from tree drafting rather than from the latent forecast alone ([ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)). Second, every lossless method that injects a future vector (ConFu, SD²) gains roughly **8% to 35% τ** and none uses it to prune. Third, every method that drafts at the step or meaning level is lossy ([Lookahead Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19830v1), [SemanticSpec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03708)).

## A shared space is the easy part, and the vocabulary already is one

The paper the idea cites as its Rosetta stone does not test what the idea needs. arXiv 2610.09411 aligns independently trained encoders, mostly vision against language, with one orthogonal map found from geometry alone and no paired data ([Rosetta stone](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)). Its quality is category-level. Cross-modal FOSCTTM averages 0.154, where 0 is perfect and 0.5 is chance, and paired CLIP reaches 0.0006. The authors state that alignment is coarse and that the paper does not analyze raw LLM hidden states. Two lessons transfer. Same-modality text encoders align almost perfectly without pairs (FOSCTTM 0.0000 on NQ), and linear CKA between two spaces predicts how alignable they are (R² 0.69 in the paper text), which makes CKA a cheap pre-check for any drafter and target pair ([Rosetta stone](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)). The unpaired machinery itself is unnecessary for speculative decoding, because running the same prompt through both models yields unlimited pairs.

Direct measurements between sizes of one family show strong but incomplete overlap, concentrated in middle layers. OpenLlama-3B and 13B reach mutual k-NN of about 0.6 at k=1 regardless of gallery size, while cross-modal alignment collapses toward zero on large galleries ([Back into Plato's Cave](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18572v1)). Gemma-2-2B and 9B share SAE-feature subspaces with **SVCCA up to 0.73 in middle layers but 0.374 at the 9B final layer** ([Semantic Convergence](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22918v1)). That is a warning for a gist defined on final-layer states. Calibrated re-analyses find that neighbourhood structure converges more reliably than metric distances ([Structure over Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27252)), which favours ranking or contrastive gist objectives over MSE on coordinates. No measurement exists for a 0.5B against an 8B pair. The largest gaps measured are 2B to 9B and 3B to 13B.

The stitching results are the most important for this idea, because they show what a map can and cannot carry. Affine maps between same-family residual streams round-trip almost losslessly through the small model, but small-to-large replacement is bottlenecked. **Gemma-2-2B to 9B stitched cross-entropy is 3.28, worse than the 2B model alone at 2.52**, and Pythia and GPT-2 pairs show +11% to +27% ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)). For Qwen2.5-0.5B to 1.5B, a small ReLU autoencoder mapper transfers steering vectors far better than an affine map (backdoor judge score 2.6 vs 0.2) at 0.32% of the 0.5B model's parameters ([Oozeer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04429v4)). Unsupervised translation is worse and unstable. vec2vec needs about 50K samples and converged in only 3 of 15 seeds for one pair ([vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)). On the drafting side, NarrowSpec shows a quarter-width drafter stream (1024 for Qwen3-8B) loses only **2% to 5% acceptance** ([NarrowSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23835v1)), so a space narrower than the target carries what drafting needs.

The practical conclusion is that the mapping is cheap and learnable, and it does not add the target's knowledge. A mapped small-model state carries roughly the small model's information. For a same-family Qwen pair there is an even simpler shared space. Qwen2.5-0.5B and 1.5B use one tokenizer, so a gist expressed as a distribution over the shared vocabulary, scored through each model's LM-head embeddings, needs no alignment at all. That is also the form a pruning mask must take in the end.

## Small models see almost as much of the future as the target does

Whether a small model can forecast a large model's future depends on how much the large model knows that the small one does not. The literature never measures that difference, but it bounds both terms. Token-level foresight is short. A learned soft-prompt decoder on GPT-J-6B recovers the token 2 to 4 positions ahead at **44% to 48% top-1, and a linear probe only 16% to 29%** ([Future Lens](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.04897)). Accuracy concentrates where the model is already confident (26% below 30% confidence, 95% above 90%), so decodable future tokens are mostly ones a drafter already gets right ([Future Lens](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.04897)). On general text, "the k=8 probe overlaps the unigram baseline at every layer" in Qwen3, Gemma-3 and Llama-3 ([Where's the Plan](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07984)). Coarse gist goes further. A linear map from Llama-3.2-3B residuals into a SONAR embedding predicts the next paragraph at **cosine 0.55, against 0.50 for five real future tokens, 0.20 for random and 0.82 for regenerating**, with 76% subject match but 3% at moderate detail ([ParaScopes](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00180v1)). The signal sits at paragraph breaks and at 60% to 80% depth. A document-level outline probe was weak. Prompt-end probes predict whole-response length, step count and answer choice, and probes saturate at 8 to 128 hidden units, so the plan is low-dimensional ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06258v3)).

Scale helps slowly, and our models sit below the documented thresholds. Rhyme-planning probe signals grow with scale, but activation patching finds causal use of the plan only in Gemma-3-27B. For Qwen3 0.6B to 8B the newline probe gap's confidence interval includes zero, and last-word causal planning appears only from 4B ([Where's the Plan](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07984)). Cross-model prediction is weaker than self-prediction. GPT-4o predicting Llama-70B's behaviour plateaued at 31.8% to 35.2% against 48.5% for Llama-70B's self-prediction, and neither predicted long-output properties such as response length or review sentiment ([Binder et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13787)). Small models can still approximate large ones at the logit level. Proxy-tuning with 7B models closes 88% of the gap to Llama2-70B-chat ([proxy-tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08565)).

Our own experiments fill the missing measurement for one pair, and the answer is discouraging for the target-information half of the idea. With a Qwen2.5-1.5B target and a 0.5B drafter on GSM8K, linear probes predicted the mean embedding of the next 32 tokens at **R² 0.37 from the target and 0.34 from the drafter**, both about twice the current-token baseline. Exact tokens beyond about 4 ahead were not linearly decodable from either model. Our attempts to feed a target plan vector into a block drafter produced no valid gain. So a drafter-side gist forecaster can reach nearly the target's own gist accuracy, which sounds good, but it means the gist carries almost nothing the drafter did not already compute.

Latent world models for text confirm that rolling a compact state forward works at short horizons and needs a distributional head. NextLat's MLP dynamics model maps (hidden state, next token) to the next hidden state. The v1 HTML reports it only as a training regularizer at 57M to 162M scale on synthetic tasks. A later version, read in full earlier in this project, pretrains it into 1.3B models and uses it for self-speculative drafting at up to 3.3x and 4.9 accepted tokens ([NextLat](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05963v1)). It needs the target retrained from scratch and is token-conditioned rather than a free-running gist forecaster. BST, JTP and Semformer train belief-state or future-latent objectives but are tested only at 57M to 162M parameters ([BST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23506), [JTP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21801v1), [Semformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11143)). CALM compresses K=4 tokens into one 128-dim vector with over 99.9% reconstruction, but quality degrades at K=8 and it is a new standalone lossy LM rather than a drafter ([CALM](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27688v1)). The JEPA Paradox paper argues that squared-error latent prediction collapses in language because text has many valid continuations ([JEPA Paradox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23531)). LCM, PLANNER and Cornille et al. all use generative heads or discrete cluster codes for sentence-level latents for this reason ([LCM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08821), [PLANNER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02531), [Cornille et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00614)). A gist used for pruning sidesteps the problem if it is a set-valued prediction, such as FSP's bag of future tokens, rather than a point in embedding space.

## Pruning pays for compute, and lossless rules cap what it can add

Lossless verification makes any candidate restriction safe. Only the drafter's proposal changes, and under sampling the drafter distribution must be renormalized over the subset and used in the rejection test. Grammar-constrained speculative decoding in TensorRT-LLM already applies context-dependent hard masks to tree drafts with per-node state and rollback ([TensorRT-LLM](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/1.3.0rc26/blogs/tech_blog/blog12_Combining_Guided_Decoding_and_Speculative_Decoding.html)), so the machinery for a gist mask exists. The question is what the mask buys.

It can buy compute, and the literature sizes that gain. Static trimming gives about **1.12x over EAGLE-2** at a cost of roughly 6% τ in independent measurements ([FR-Spec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14856), [EvoSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27390v2)). Context-dependent methods close most of the acceptance gap. DynaSpec keeps 98.4% of full-vocabulary τ, and NanoSpec reaches 2.25x against 2.12x for FR-Spec with an active set under 3k tokens and no training ([DynaSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13847), [NanoSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26444)). ChainFlow's forecast-based cut gave +13% throughput ([ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)). The gain is bounded by the LM head's share of drafter time. For Qwen2.5-0.5B, our arithmetic from the public config (151,936 vocabulary, width 896) puts the head at about 136M of 494M parameters, or roughly a quarter of drafter compute per token. Pruning it fully would save about a quarter of draft time, and less end to end because a 1.5B target's verification dominates.

It can buy acceptance only in a narrow way. A hard mask changes the drafter's choice only when its preferred token falls outside the mask. Each such event is either a fix, where the masked choice matches the target, or a break, where the drafter was right and the mask removed the correct token. If the mask is computed from the drafter's own states, it holds no information the drafter lacks, so any net fix comes from the gist head having been trained on target continuations with a different objective. Plain distillation of the drafter targets the same deficit more directly, with documented gains of 10% to 45% ([DistillSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08461)). If the mask is computed from target states, our R² gap of 0.03 bounds the extra information, and the target states at the last verified position are free in speculative decoding anyway, so a target-fed drafter such as DFlash already sees them. The tree-pruning side tells the same story. Confidence-based and classifier-based pruning (EAGLE-2, OPT-Tree, C2T, SpecDec++) already cuts breadth and depth, and C2T argues EAGLE-2's dynamic tree adds only 11% acceptance at matched tree size ([C2T](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13652), [SpecDec++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19715)).

The sensitivity is steep in both directions. With per-token acceptance near 0.8 and a 16-token block, one point of acceptance moves τ by about 4% to 5% (our calculation from the geometric acceptance model). A mask that misses the target's token 0.5% of the time therefore costs about 2% τ, and FR-Spec's 8k-token ablation already showed acceptance falling noticeably at small static sets ([FR-Spec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14856)). A gist mask has to be both small and nearly perfect in recall to pay for itself.

## What remains open, and how novel it is

Four specific pieces were not found in any lossless work. No drafter compresses many future tokens (16 to hundreds) into one gist and predicts it. ChainFlow compresses channels per token, and CALM's K-token vectors are not used for drafting. No method conditions vocabulary or tree pruning on a forecast of upcoming content. DynaSpec, SpecVocab and NanoSpec route from current state or recent history, and ChainFlow's candidate cut uses an 8-token per-token forecast as an efficiency trick it never analyzes. No method amortizes one span-level candidate set over a whole draft block of 16 or more positions, which would remove DynaSpec's per-step router cost. No one uses an independent small LM's own forecast, mapped into a space shared with the target, to steer drafting. SD² is the nearest, but its vector flows from the target to the drafter.

The candid judgment is that novelty is real but thin, and promise is low for acceptance and modest for efficiency. The combination is a recombination of ChainFlow, DynaSpec or NanoSpec, and FSP, and reviewers will read it that way. The acceptance case is weak on first principles and on our data. A drafter-side forecast adds no information, the target adds almost none at this scale (R² 0.37 vs 0.34), EAGLE-3 found future-feature regression harmful, and our plan-vector experiments found no gain. The efficiency case is plausible but small. ChainFlow's +13% and NanoSpec's training-free result set the scale and the bar. The setting where the idea would matter most is a large vocabulary with a small drafter, such as Qwen's 151,936 tokens with a 0.5B drafter, where the LM head is a quarter of draft compute and a once-per-block mask amortizes well. A span-level mask could also help one-pass block drafters coordinate positions, but that coupling job is already handled by sampler heads and Markov heads ([ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669), [Apple 2507.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11851)). A paper here would be an efficiency paper with an honest negative result on acceptance, not a new paradigm. The search was web-based with patchy 2026 indexing and no citation chase from ChainFlow, DynaSpec or FSP, so a closer paper could exist.

## One-hour experiment with a kill number

The cheapest test needs no drafting engine and fits on one 12 GB GPU in bf16, since Qwen2.5-0.5B and 1.5B together take under 5 GB. Generate greedy Qwen2.5-1.5B continuations of up to 256 tokens for GSM8K (300 prompts), HumanEval (164) and MT-Bench (80, both turns), which takes minutes. Run one teacher-forced pass of each model over those sequences and cache the 0.5B drafter's logits plus hidden states from both models at the final layer and at about 70% depth. Train a gist head that scores every vocabulary token as sigmoid(e_v · W h), where e_v is the frozen shared LM-head embedding and only W is learned, with binary cross-entropy against the multi-hot set of the next 32 target tokens. This is FSP's bag-of-words target ([FSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14751)) and trains in minutes. Fit one head on drafter states and one on target states at the last verified position.

At the start of each 16-token block, build candidate sets of size K = 512, 2048 and 4096 from five sources. These are a static frequency set (FR-Spec), a NanoSpec-style recency set of prompt tokens, target top-3 at prompt positions and tokens generated so far, the drafter-state gist head, the target-state gist head, and an oracle that pads the true next-16 tokens with gist-ranked fill as a ceiling. Report two numbers per source and offset. The first is recall, the fraction of positions where the target's actual token is in the set. The second is the net change in drafter top-1 agreement with the target when the drafter's logits are masked to the set, split into fixes and breaks. Convert agreement to τ with a free-running masked greedy simulation of the 0.5B drafter against the cached target tokens as a final check. A second stage, only if the first passes, repeats the analysis on Qwen3.5-0.8B against Qwen3.5-9B in 4-bit (about 5 to 6 GB) to test whether a larger gap gives the target more to contribute.

The go/no-go numbers follow from the sensitivity above. **The acceptance path passes only if the drafter-state gist mask raises top-1 agreement by at least +1.5 points at K ≤ 4096 on at least two of the three tasks**, which corresponds to roughly +7% τ at block 16 and matches the low end of ConFu's gain. It must also beat a soft version that adds the gist log-score as a logit bias, and a drafter fine-tuned by plain distillation for the same steps, since a gain that distillation also produces is not the gist's. **The efficiency path passes only if recall reaches at least 99.5% at K = 2048**, which holds the τ loss near 2% while cutting the head by about 98%. Recall must also exceed the NanoSpec-style set at equal K, because a training-free set that matches it makes the gist redundant. **Kill the idea if net agreement change is at or below +0.5 points and recall does not beat the recency set.** The prediction from everything above is that the acceptance test fails, with fixes and breaks roughly cancelling and the target-state head barely ahead of the drafter-state head. The efficiency test is likely to pass on GSM8K and HumanEval, where vocabulary is repetitive, and fail on MT-Bench.

## Conclusion

The idea's appealing parts, a world-model-like forecaster and a Rosetta-stone shared space, turn out to be the parts that matter least. The shared space is a solved, cheap mapping problem, and for same-tokenizer pairs it is the vocabulary itself. The forecaster can be built, but at 0.5B to 1.5B scale it forecasts what the drafter already knows. What survives is pruning, and pruning in lossless decoding is an accounting trick on drafter compute whose ceiling is the LM head's share of draft time. That reframing has a practical upside. The decisive measurement is offline, costs about a GPU-hour, and reuses the probe pipeline we already have.

If the experiment passes only on efficiency, the right follow-up is a once-per-block gist shortlist for a DFlash-style drafter with Qwen's 151k vocabulary, benchmarked against NanoSpec and ChainFlow's candidate cut on wall-clock time, with an exactness test at T=1. If it fails on both counts, the result is still useful as a direct measurement that drafter-side gist forecasts carry no exploitable information beyond the drafter's own next-token distribution. Together with our probe results and the failed plan-vector runs, that would close this line at this model scale.
