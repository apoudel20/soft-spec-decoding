# Target gists can steer draft trees

Of the three parts of the design, one is open and testable, one fails on its own logic, and one needs training before it can work. **No published lossless method uses a target-side forward-looking signal at inference time to decide which branches of a draft tree get grown.** Every tree builder found allocates budget from drafter confidence, offline acceptance statistics or hardware cost. The target-derived methods either act only at training time (ExitTrain, 2610.11750), feed the signal to the drafter rather than the allocator (ConFu, 2603.08899, and SD², 2511.09844), or use only the target's next-token distribution to rerank first-token branches (2610.29210). So part (1), a Qwen3.5-9B gist read from specific layers, heads or SAE features that sets the tree's shape, is an unoccupied slot. It is lossless by construction, because it changes only what is proposed. Part (2) does not survive as stated. Under exact verification the next round's context always leaves the verified tree at the first miss, so the target's KV for unchosen branches can never be reused. 2610.29210 states the general form, that exact reuse "requires a distribution computed at the next round's complete context". What can usefully persist is drafter-side work for tokens never sent to the target, and on one GPU its payoff is capped by the drafter's share of round time. Part (3), a two-sided tree in latent space with a co-trained target and drafter, has no precedent in exact decoding. Its ingredients exist (tree-masked training, target-exposed future latents, exact tree verification). But training-free superposed inputs collapse to the top token, which matches our own null result for target-side soft inputs. The realistic prize is the size of ConFu's and ExitTrain's gains, about **+8% to +14% acceptance length**. The decisive cheap test is an offline oracle measurement of how much tree-allocation headroom exists on Qwen3.5-9B with a DFlash drafter, which needs no tree kernel and fits on a 12 GB card.

Throughout, τ is mean accepted tokens per target forward pass and "x" is wall-clock speedup over autoregressive decoding. "Lossless" means the output distribution equals the target's, or the tokens are identical under greedy decoding. Our own prior results are labeled as such and have no external citation. Statements marked "guess" are priors or arithmetic, not findings.

The crossover numbers depend on where the weights live. On a datacenter GPU with weights resident, SwiftSpec measured a Llama3-70B target pass at **10.32 ms for 4 tokens, 10.39 ms for 8 and 13.25 ms for 16**, about +29%, with no gain below 8 because 8 is the smallest tensor-core tile ([SwiftSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11309v1)). A roofline estimate puts the H100 ridge near **300 FLOP/byte**, so batch times verified tokens above about 300 becomes compute-bound ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/ji_ai/speculative-decoding-why-80-acceptance-still-loses-at-batch-64-4pd4), a blog, not peer reviewed). An energy-accounting study with hardware counters found that the verified-token count, not the drafter, sets the crossover. Verifying 16 tokens crosses the H200 balance point at batch 16, and **DFlash-16 accepts under 3 of its 16 verified tokens** in that study ([2610.40287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40287)). Offloading flips the picture. SpecExec's optimal budget is **1024 to 2048 tokens in fp16 but 128 to 256 for 4-bit GPTQ**, because PCIe transfer dominates and fewer bytes per weight shrink the free budget ([SpecExec](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02532)).

No source gives a latency-versus-tree-size curve for a consumer GPU with weights on the card. Guess, from public spec sheets not checked this session: an RTX 4070 Ti has about 504 GB/s of memory bandwidth and on the order of 80 TFLOP/s of dense FP16 tensor throughput with FP32 accumulation, a ridge near 150 FLOP/byte. A bf16 weight gives 1 FLOP per byte per token, and a 4-bit weight gives about 4, so the knee for 4-bit Qwen3.5-9B would sit near 30 to 40 tokens on paper. Dequantization kernels usually lower that, and some 4-bit backends handle multi-token batches badly. The working prior is that the free budget is **tens of nodes, not hundreds**, which is the regime where 2610.29210 found that better allocation helps most. That makes allocation more valuable on this card, not less. This must be measured first.

Qwen3.5-9B adds a complication the notes flag but no paper solves for inference. It is a hybrid with Gated DeltaNet layers in a 3:1 pattern with full attention ([Raschka gallery](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/hybrid-attention/), stated for Qwen3-Next). The recurrent layers have no KV cache to branch, so each tree node needs its own state. Tree Training adapts state-space layers so log-probs over a serialized prefix tree match per-branch computation, but only for training ([2511.00413](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00413v4)). ChainFlow ran greedy tree verification on Qwen3.5 only after patching the engine, and it reports that **4% to 13% of prompts diverge from plain greedy decoding** through fp16 near-ties ([ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)). Guess: the attention KV per token is small for this model because only about 8 of 32 layers have KV, while a DeltaNet state snapshot is a fixed matrix per head per layer and likely costs megabytes per node. The real memory cost of branching is therefore state snapshots at branch points, not KV. Check `layer_types` and the head sizes in `config.json` before relying on this. This also argues for keeping the first experiments offline.

## Kept branches pay only on the drafter side

Almost every method discards the target's KV for rejected branches after each round. Persistence exists in weaker forms. SwiftSpec and SSD keep drafter work. SpecExec keeps target outputs, but only inside one large tree. Token Recycling and SuffixDecoding keep token IDs without KV. I found no method that keeps the target's KV for rejected or unexplored branches alive across rounds, and the reason is structural.

The published constraint comes from 2610.29210. **Exact reuse of a target distribution is guaranteed only when it was computed at the next round's complete context.** Keeping such a distribution can require giving up accepted tokens, and the paper gives conditions under which that loss persists across rounds ([2610.29210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29210)). Causal attention makes a node's KV and next-token distribution a function of its exact ancestor path, so a kept branch is valid only if the accepted context is literally its ancestor path.

Applying that constraint to the design gives a sharper result. This is our own derivation, not a published theorem, and it should be checked before it goes into a paper. An exact verifier walks the tree along the target's own choices and stops at a node u where the target's next token e is not an available child. Under greedy decoding, e is the target's argmax and the walk continues whenever the argmax is a child. Under single-draft or recursive rejection sampling, the residual distribution puts zero mass on every rejected child, because a token is rejected only where the target probability is below the draft probability. So e can never be a verified-and-rejected child. Under SpecExec-style verification, which samples from the target and checks membership, the walk continues while the sample is in the tree. In every case the next context is the accepted path plus a token outside the verified tree. Every target-computed node off that path is off every future context, because exact decoding never backtracks. Kept target KV therefore never gets a "later match". The only exception is a node the policy chose not to walk, which is the accepted-token sacrifice 2610.29210 describes. I did not check optimal-transport verifiers such as SpecTr, where the residual might cover a rejected child. If it does, the fix is to keep walking in the same round, not to persist across rounds.

What survives is a large drafter-side tree with a small verified subset. If the drafter proposed e at node u but the token was never sent to the target, the drafter's subtree under e is ready when the round ends. That is SSD's outcome-keyed speculation cache and SwiftSpec's re-rooting. SSD reports the bonus token is predictable up to about 90% of the time and that its gain comes from overlapping drafting with verification on separate hardware ([SSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03251v3)). SwiftSpec does not report its re-root hit rate ([SwiftSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11309v1)). On one GPU nothing overlaps, so persistence saves at most the next drafter pass. Its ceiling is hit rate times the drafter's share of round time. A DFlash drafter adds a second limit. It conditions on target hidden states at the last verified position, so a kept subtree was drafted from stale conditioning. It remains a valid proposal if verification uses the probabilities it was actually sampled under, but its quality is lower.

The memory side is cheap and the bandwidth side is not. KV for Llama-3-8B is 128 KiB per token, so a 1000-node forest is about 125 MiB (arithmetic in the notes from public configs). DeFT shows the right kernel property, loading shared-prefix KV once, which cuts KV IO by 73% to 99% ([DeFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00242v4)). On consumer cards the practical kernels are Triton (DeFT) and FlashInfer's non-Hopper paths. No consumer-GPU benchmark of either was found.

ExitTrain is the work a reviewer will cite against part (1). It starts from the same diagnosis, that draft-score builders "spend budget on branches the target rarely follows", and fixes it by training the drafter with target-derived exit weights. It beats token-only fine-tuning by about 5.9% τ on Qwen3-4B ([ExitTrain](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11750)). Its correction is amortized into drafter weights. An inference-time gist that changes per context is a different claim, and it must beat ExitTrain-style training, not only EAGLE-2-style allocation. GRAFT shows that a target-derived prior can reshape a tree at about 0.3 ms per round, so a light gist scorer is affordable on overhead grounds ([GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20375)). Both ConFu and SD² stay lossless because the signal changes only proposals, and any gist-steered allocation inherits that property as long as the gist comes from already-verified context and not from the verification coins.

Multi-draft exact sampling is a smaller lever than tree shape. The best verifier adds a few acceptance points and **about 1.0x to 1.17x wall clock inside an EAGLE tree**, and drawing drafts without replacement is the main gain ([2502.18779](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18779)). At T=0 the whole apparatus reduces to whether the target's argmax is among the candidates, so steering is then purely about putting the argmax path inside the tree. At T>0 the steering target should be probability mass, as ExitTrain's uncovered-mass term is, not the argmax.

Our block-drafter data says where steering would have to act. Per-position conditional acceptance on GSM8K fell **from 0.90 at offset 1 to 0.53 at offset 8** (our result). Multiplying these out, the expected accepted drafts in an 8-token chain are about 3.7, and the first rejection is spread fairly evenly over offsets 3 to 6 (each about 12% to 16%). One extra branch helps only if it sits at the offset where this round will fail, and that offset changes per round. A target signal that predicts the first-rejection offset, and which of the drafter's runners-up the target will choose there, is exactly what an allocator needs. That is a narrower and more testable target than "the gist".

The search found no lossless two-level tree in which semantic branches carry token subtrees. Lookahead Reasoning and ConfSpec branch at the step level and accept semantically equivalent text, so they are lossy ([Lookahead Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19830v1), [ConfSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18447v1)). A lossless version is easy to define. The gist selects a few continuation modes, each mode gets a token subtree, and verification stays token-exact. It would capture only the coverage part of Lookahead Reasoning's gain, not the part from accepting non-identical steps.

## Qwen's plan is decodable at boundaries but rarely causal

The literature agrees on where to look and disagrees with itself on what the signal means. Future information is reliably decodable at boundary tokens (end of prompt, "\n", "\n\n") in middle-to-late layers, and decodability grows with scale. Causal use of that boundary encoding is rare, and Qwen is the documented negative case.

The Qwen result matters most. In rhyme planning, Qwen3 at every size from 0.6B to 32B shows strong probe signal at the newline but near-zero causal effect there. Newline patching stays at or below 7% while last-word patching rises to 53% at 8B ([2605.07984](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07984)). For a drafter or an allocator that is acceptable, since only decodability is needed. It does mean patching results should not choose the readout site for Qwen. The split-prefill finding that Qwen3.5-4B's recurrent state, not its KV cache, controls continuation mode is suggestive but rests on a search snippet whose PDF could not be parsed ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.2061.pdf)). If it holds, the DeltaNet states at the end of the prompt are a gist site worth probing alongside the residual stream. Head-level search applies only to the full-attention layers, which are the Qwen analogue of Gemma's 28.x and 30.x plan heads.

Abliteration-style difference-of-means directions are the cheapest causal instrument available. Arditi et al. show that erasing a single residual direction, found from contrasting activations on two prompt sets, removes refusal across 13 models up to 72B, and adding it back induces refusal ([2406.11717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)). Marks and Tegmark find that difference-in-means probes generalize as well as other probes and pick out directions more causally implicated in outputs ([2310.06824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06824)). The same estimator applied to gist contrasts would give one direction per contrast. Examples are "next segment is an equation vs prose", "answer imminent vs more steps", and "code vs explanation". Each costs a few hundred labeled positions and one mean subtraction. Ablating the direction, by projecting it out of the residual stream from the chosen layer onward, then tests whether the target's future depends on it. The reading is two-sided. A direction that is decodable and whose ablation changes the continuation marks a decision the target has already made, the strongest kind of steering signal. A direction that is decodable but inert is still usable for allocation, but it is more likely a prompt-topic shortcut, so it needs a prompt-embedding baseline before anyone trusts it. Given the Qwen3 patching results, the prior is that most gist directions in Qwen3.5-9B will be inert at boundaries. That prior is borrowed from rhyme planning in Qwen3 and has not been measured on Qwen3.5.

The 12 GB pipeline follows from the notes. A 4-bit 9B model takes about 5 to 6 GB. One Qwen-Scope layer is two 65,536 by 4096 matrices, about 0.54 GB each in bf16, so SAEs are loaded one or two layers at a time and run on cached activations offline. Caching residuals for 5,000 prompts at 10 positions and 32 layers in fp16 is about 13 GB on disk. Ridge and logistic probes over (layer, position) take minutes. Transcoders and circuit-tracer are out of reach for Qwen3.5-9B. No transcoders are published for it, and the hybrid layers are unlikely to be supported. Qwen3-4B with public transcoders is the feasible proxy if attribution graphs become necessary ([Neuronpedia](https://www.neuronpedia.org/qwen3-4b/10-transcoder-hp)). The SAEs were trained on the base model, so FVU must be measured on instruct, 4-bit activations before any feature ranking is trusted.

Our own numbers set expectations for the target's edge. With a Qwen2.5-1.5B target and a 0.5B drafter, linear probes for the mean embedding of the next 32 tokens reached **R² 0.37 from the target and 0.34 from the drafter** (our result). The size ratio there was 3x. Qwen3.5-9B against Qwen3.5-0.8B is about 11x, and scale helps both pre-caching and planning ([2404.00859](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00859), [2502.06258](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06258)), so the gap should be larger. How much larger is a guess until measured. A DFlash drafter already reads fused target features, so the relevant comparison is the target's gist against what DFlash's own conditioning already carries, not against a standalone 0.8B model.

Two facts constrain the latent version. First, training-free soft inputs to pretrained models collapse onto the top token, the "Greedy Pitfall" ([2508.03440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03440v4)). That matches our own finding that target-side superposed inputs did not beat hard guesses. Real multi-path behaviour appears only after training, as in Soft Tokens, Hard Truths and Multiplex Thinking ([2509.19170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19170), [2601.08808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08808)). Second, drafter-side soft feedback did help us, by about 10% τ (our result), though most of it came from soft supervision in general and shrank to 1% to 4% with target TV labels. The asymmetry is sensible. The drafter is trained, so it learns to use a mixture, while the frozen target is not. A target-side gist tree therefore requires training the target, or at least a ConFu-style side channel, to emit a branchable latent.

The symbiotic version is the least explored. Apple's gated LoRA shows a target can be fine-tuned to expose future tokens without changing its own outputs ([2507.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11851v1)). Production MTP shows joint training of target and draft head ([DeepSeek-V3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.19437)). Tree Training and ThreadWeaver show tree-masked training is routine infrastructure, including for state-space layers ([Tree Training](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00413v4)). No paper found trains a target to emit a branched or multimodal future latent for a drafter, or adds a drafter-acceptance term to the target's loss while keeping outputs fixed. One conceptual point limits the whole two-sided idea at T=0. Under greedy decoding the target has exactly one future, so a target-side gist tree can only hedge against estimation error, not represent real alternatives. Branching on the target side has content only under sampling, and how often the target's sampled futures are genuinely multimodal at segment boundaries has not been measured.

## What is precisely open, and how promising it is

Six things were not found in any lossless work. First, inference-time tree allocation driven by a target-side forward-looking signal deeper than the next token, such as a gist, a predicted first-rejection offset or a plan feature. The nearest are 2610.29210 (target next-token distribution, depth one), ExitTrain (training time) and ConFu (conditioning only). Second, using specific target layers, heads, SAE features or difference-of-means directions as that signal, with causal validation by ablation. Third, a lossless two-level tree in which a few gist modes each own a token subtree. Fourth, an exact branched-state verifier for hybrid DeltaNet models at inference. ChainFlow's patched greedy engine is not exact. Fifth, a target trained to emit a branchable future latent while its own outputs stay fixed. Sixth, a measurement of how often a drafter's unsent candidates contain the round's exit token on a single GPU. That last one is the honest replacement for kept target branches, which our derivation says cannot be reused.

The candid assessment separates the parts. Part (1) is the strongest. It is lossless for free, it lands where the field's own diagnosis (ExitTrain) says budget is wasted, the gist signal rides on a verification pass that already happens, and the small tree budget a 4070 Ti likely allows is where allocation matters most. Against it, ExitTrain already captures much of the gain in drafter weights. DFlash already conditions on target features. And at our earlier scale the target's gist edge over the drafter was R² 0.03. The realistic upside is the ConFu and ExitTrain range of +8% to +14% τ, with a guessed wall-clock gain of +5% to +10% after overhead. That is a solid workshop or conference contribution if the 9B-to-0.8B gap turns out larger than 1.5B-to-0.5B. It is not a new paradigm. Part (2) as kept target KV is closed by exactness. As a drafter-side cache it is real but belongs to SSD's multi-device setting, and on one GPU it is bounded by the drafter's share of time. Part (3) is the most interesting scientifically and the most expensive. Its first cheap question is whether there is anything to branch on at all, since greedy decoding has no alternatives and frozen targets collapse superpositions.

## A staged plan for one RTX 4070 Ti

Every stage below runs on one 12 GB card with Qwen3.5-9B in 4-bit and either z-lab/Qwen3.5-9B-DFlash or Qwen3.5-0.8B as the drafter. Stages 1 to 6 are offline and need no tree kernel. Branches are scored by teacher-forcing them as separate sequences, which avoids the unsolved hybrid tree-verification problem until the numbers justify it. Durations are guesses. Each stage has a go/no-go number, and a "kill" closes that branch of the idea.

| Stage | What to measure | Go | Kill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Hardware knee (half day) | Target forward latency for 1 to 128 new tokens at contexts 512 and 2048 in two 4-bit backends, plus DFlash drafter pass time and its share of a round | Free budget B* (largest n with latency at most 1.25x of n=1) is at least 16 | B* below 8, so trees cannot pay on this card. Continue offline for the proposal only |
| 1. Oracle allocation headroom (1 day) | Build 256-node candidate trees from DFlash per-position top-k and from 0.8B best-first. Compare τ at budgets 8 to 64 for drafter-score allocation, 2610.29210 first-token reranking, and an oracle that knows the target's greedy path | Oracle beats drafter allocation by at least 20% τ at B ≤ B* | Headroom under 10%. Then no allocator can matter, stop part (1) |
| 2. Readout sweep (1 to 2 days) | Probes per (layer, position) on Qwen3.5-9B residuals, full-attention head outputs and end-of-prompt DeltaNet states. Targets are next-32 bag of tokens, mean next-32 embedding, first-rejection offset of the next DFlash block, and which runner-up the target picks there. Same probes on DFlash and 0.8B states. Directional ablation and addition, scoring KL on the next 32 tokens and gist-class flip rate | At least one direction with held-out AUROC at least 0.80 whose ablation flips the continuation's gist class in at least 30% of cases. Report as a negative causal result and lean on dense probes |
| 4. Gist-guided allocation, offline (1 day) | Small scorer for node acceptance from drafter node features, with and without the best target gist features from stages 2 and 3, allocating by expected gain | Captures at least 30% of stage-1 headroom and at least +5% τ over the best non-gist allocator, including the 2610.29210 rule | Gain over the same scorer without gist features under 2%, meaning capacity, not the gist, did the work |
| 5. Persistence hit rate (half day) | How often the exit token is among the drafter's unsent candidates at the exit node, and the share of round time a hit would save | Hit rate times drafter share at least 8% of round time | Below 8%. | 6. | 8. The ordering puts the cheapest kill first. Stage 1 bounds everything in part (1) before any probe is trained, and stage 4's no-gist control prevents a better scorer from being credited to the gist, the same trap our earlier report flagged for distillation. If stages 1, 2 and 4 pass, the case for a larger compute request is concrete. It would cover three things. The first is training stage-7 gist emitters on Qwen3.5-9B and 27B in bf16. The second is a two-GPU SSD-style setup where drafter-side persistence can overlap verification. The third is a proper branched-state kernel for hybrid models, measured against ExitTrain, DDTree and ChainFlow on the same targets. If stage 1 fails, the result is still publishable as a direct measurement that tree allocation on small consumer budgets has little headroom left above drafter confidence.

## Conclusion

The design changes shape once exactness is applied. Branch persistence looked like the systems contribution and gist steering like the speculative one. In fact the target's unchosen branches are provably dead under exact verification, while steering is the part with a clean lossless story and a measurable ceiling. The most useful reframing is to stop asking the target for a general gist and ask it two narrow questions an allocator can use. Where will this round's draft fail, and which runner-up will the target take there? Those are things a target knows about itself and a DFlash drafter conditioned on the last verified state may not. They are also what ExitTrain approximates offline.

The 12 GB constraint helps the science more than it hurts. Small free tree budgets are where allocation quality matters most, and the offline oracle, probe and ablation stages need only cached activations and teacher-forced scoring. The whole go/no-go chain through stage 6 costs about a week of single-GPU time. That is cheap enough that the honest default is to run it before arguing further about whether target foresight at 9B is large enough to matter.
