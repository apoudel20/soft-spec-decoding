# Geometry of LLM hidden states for a latent autoencoder / diffusion drafter (Qwen3-8B, layers 18/24/30/36)

Scope: whether a learned autoencoder or diffusion model over frozen Qwen3-8B hidden states would be smooth and decodable, with the final-layer state decoded by the frozen LM head. Research done 2026-11-23. Items marked [UNVERIFIED] come from memory or from snippets I could not open, and need checking before use.

Basic target facts: Qwen3-8B has 36 layers and hidden size 4096 — [DiHAL, arXiv 2605.14368](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14368v2). Qwen3 applies a final RMSNorm before the LM head, so the decoded token depends only on the direction of the final residual vector (after elementwise gain), not its scale [UNVERIFIED this session, standard Qwen/Llama architecture, check `modeling_qwen3.py`].

## Q1. Massive activations, outlier dimensions, attention sinks, anisotropy; Qwen specifics; normalization fixes

### Takeaway
Qwen3-8B has massive activations (global max |activation| 17,664, against a median of about 3 at the peak token). In other models these sit in a few fixed dimensions on the first token and delimiters. They act like near-constant biases that feed attention sinks and RMSNorm rescaling. Raw MSE over layers 18/24/30 would be dominated by a handful of coordinates on a few tokens. Those coordinates should be modelled as constants or masked out, and the remaining dimensions standardized.

### Cited Findings
- Sun et al., "Massive Activations in Large Language Models", arXiv 2402.17762 (COLM 2024). A very small number of activations are up to about 100,000x larger than typical. LLaMA2-7B top-1 is 2622.0, top-2 1547.0, median 0.2. LLaMA2-13B top-1 is 1264.0. Mixtral-8x7B top-1 is 7100.0. LLaMA2-70B exceeds 15,000 — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Same paper: they sit in fixed feature dimensions (LLaMA2-7B dims 1415 and 2533, LLaMA2-13B 2100 and 4743, Mixtral 2070 and 3398). They appear abruptly in an early layer (layer 2 in LLaMA2-7B, layer 4 in 13B), stay nearly constant until about layer 30, and fade in the last few layers — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Same paper: the carrier tokens are the starting token and the first "." or "\n" (LLaMA2-7B), only the starting token (LLaMA2-13B), or the start, delimiters and words like "and"/"of" (Mixtral) — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Same paper: zeroing 4 massive activations in LLaMA2-7B takes WikiText PPL from 5.47 to inf and zero-shot accuracy from 68.95% to 36.75%. Setting them to their mean leaves PPL at 5.47 (C4 7.85 to 7.86, PG-19 8.57 to 8.59, accuracy 68.94%). For LLaMA2-13B, zeroing gives PPL 5729 and mean-setting gives 4.88 (unchanged). So massive activations behave as fixed biases, and the model is extremely sensitive to their value but needs nothing beyond their constant — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Same paper: massive activations concentrate attention onto their tokens (attention sinks, extending Xiao et al. 2023). An explicit attention bias in GPT-2 removes them at equal validation PPL (3.04) — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Chen et al., "Measuring Maximum Activations in Open LLMs", arXiv 2605.15572 (v2 June 2026), 24 checkpoints. Global max |activation|: Qwen3-8B 17,664 (median |a| at the peak token 3.14, passes the 1000x massive criterion), Qwen3-1.7B 14,208, Qwen3-32B 35,328, Qwen3-30B-A3B (MoE) 1,512, Qwen2.5-7B 13,248, Qwen2.5-32B 30,848. Qwen2.5 follows a "jump-and-plateau" layer profile (sharp rise early or mid, then high over a long span). No layer label or dimension indices are given for Qwen3. The residual stream holds the global max in 22 of 24 checkpoints. Qwen3.5 largely suppresses them. MoE peaks are 14 to 23x lower than matched dense models — [arXiv 2605.15572](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15572)
- "A Unified View of Attention and Residual Sinks", arXiv 2601.22966. In Qwen3-235B-A22B the first token has activations above 1000 in dims 1806 and 1423. Dim 1423 is also persistently large across most tokens (a "residual sink", a vertical stripe). The proposed mechanism, "outlier-driven rescaling", has the outliers interact with RMSNorm to rescale the non-outlier components. The RMSNorm gain on outlier dims is much smaller than the mean (example 0.006 vs 1). Removing normalization or clipping outliers hurts performance. Absorbing outliers into a learnable pre-norm scale (PreAffine) or GatedNorm helps. No Qwen3-8B numbers are given — [arXiv 2601.22966](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22966v1)
- Super Experts (arXiv 2507.23279): in Qwen3-30B-A3B, rare extreme outliers in a few experts' down_proj outputs produce the massive activations between decoder layers, and pruning 3 such experts sharply raises PPL — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23279) (snippet only)
- Timkey & van Schijndel, "All Bark and No Bite", arXiv 2109.04404 (EMNLP 2021). In BERT and GPT-2, 1 to 3 "rogue dimensions" dominate cosine similarity and Euclidean distance, and these are not the dimensions that matter for model behavior. Anisotropy is driven by these few dimensions rather than being a global property. Per-dimension standardization (z-scoring) fixes similarity measures — [arXiv 2109.04404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04404)
- Gao et al. (OpenAI), "Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders", arXiv 2406.04093. Before SAE training they subtract the mean over d_model and normalize inputs to unit norm. Residual activations were taken after layernorm, with LN weights folded in — [arXiv 2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04093v1)
- Tuned lens (arXiv 2303.08112): Pythia-12B layer 4 introduces two outlier dimensions, which the paper links to representational drift that hurts the raw logit lens — [arXiv 2303.08112](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.08112v6)

### Inferences
- Layers 18, 24 and 30 of Qwen3-8B very likely carry massive activations on the BOS/first token and some delimiter tokens. This is inferred from the jump-and-plateau profile and from Sun's "fade only in last layers". The 17,664 value is a global max and its layer is not reported. Final-layer (post-36) states should have much smaller outliers, but the last-layer magnitudes still need checking directly.
- Because mean-substitution of massive activations is harmless (Sun Table 3), the drafter does not need to model them. They can be copied as per-dimension constants (or per carrier-token constants) and excluded from the reconstruction and diffusion loss. An error of even 1% on a coordinate of about 17,000 is larger than the whole norm of typical coordinates, so including them in an unweighted MSE would swamp the signal.
- The sink and first-token positions should be excluded from statistics (mean, std, PCA) and from training targets. A drafter normally predicts only new positions, so it never needs to synthesize the first-token state.
- For the final layer, the right geometry is the one the LM head sees, RMSNorm(h)·g. Regressing in that normalized space (or with a direction/cosine loss plus a separate norm term) matches what decoding depends on.

### Gaps
- No source found giving Qwen3-8B's massive-activation dimension indices, carrier tokens or per-layer profile. This takes about an hour to measure by hooking layers 18/24/30/36 on a few hundred prompts.
- No anisotropy measurement (mean pairwise cosine) found for Qwen3. Classic anisotropy papers (Ethayarajh 2019; Gao et al. 2019 "representation degeneration", arXiv 1907.12009) were not fetched this session [UNVERIFIED].

## Q2. Sensitivity of the next token to perturbations of the final hidden state; logit/tuned lens; quantization flips; near-ties

### Takeaway
At the final layer, decoding is linear after RMSNorm, so a token flips exactly when a perturbation's projection on (W_top1 − W_top2) exceeds the top-1/top-2 logit margin. Flip rate therefore concentrates on low-margin (high-entropy) positions. The best empirical link between distributional distance and top-1 agreement is total variation: flip rate ≈ TV (median ratio 1.05). KL is a poor proxy. Middle layers decode poorly through the raw LM head (logit lens), and need a per-layer affine translator (tuned lens) to be read.

### Cited Findings
- Felicio, "How Divergence Becomes Decision Flips in Compressed Language Models", arXiv 2610.00694 (802 compressed or perturbed copies of 19 models, 5 corpora, 9 perturbation families). Top-1 flip rate tracks teacher-forced TV with median ratio 1.05 and no fitted constant. KL relates to flips through a square root and a factor that varies about 4x across models and corpora. KL mis-ranks compressors (whose flip rates differ by at least 10%) 11% of the time vs 1% for TV. In vLLM, teacher-forced TV predicts greedy speculative-decoding draft acceptance with mean relative error 1.1 to 2.4% — [arXiv 2610.00694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00694) (abstract only, full PDF unreadable this session)
- Quantized conversational models (arXiv 2407.03051): a 4-bit model has a narrower average top-1/top-2 probability margin than 16-bit, so flips happen when quantization error exceeds the margin — [arXiv 2407.03051](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03051) (search snippet)
- Quantization bias study (arXiv 2602.06181): high-uncertainty responses are 3 to 11x more likely to change under PTQ than confident ones — [arXiv 2602.06181](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06181) (search snippet)
- Blog-level claim: token-flip rates at 88k context range from about 1% to about 50% depending on quantization method, not bit width alone. Not verifiable against a primary source — [mer.vin](https://mer.vin/news/local-llm-quantization-benchmarks-reveal-silent-tool-call-failures/) [UNVERIFIED]
- Belrose et al., tuned lens, arXiv 2303.08112. The logit lens is biased (roughly 4 to 5 bits KL to the final output across most layers of GPT-Neo-2.7B) and fails to give interpretable predictions before layer 21 in GPT-Neo-2.7B. The tuned lens learns per-layer affine maps A_ℓ h + b_ℓ (d×d, identity-initialized) before the final norm and unembedding. It is more predictive and less biased than the logit lens on models up to 20B. Its features match the model's own (Spearman 0.89 influence correlation, Pythia-410M layer 18). The authors note their original SGD lenses were "severely undertrained", and Muon-trained lenses reach much lower KL — [arXiv 2303.08112](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.08112v6)
- Gupta et al., "How Do LLMs Use Their Depth?", arXiv 2510.18871 (GPT2-XL, Pythia-6.9B, Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, tuned lens). Early layers propose high-frequency tokens, and 60 to 80% of early top-1 guesses are overturned by the final layer. Determiners reach rank 1 around layer 5. Content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) do so near layer 20 (of 32). The first token of multi-token fact answers appears around layer 25 — [arXiv 2510.18871](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18871v1)
- No clean per-layer top-1-agreement curve exists for Qwen. In Qwen2.5-Omni, logit-lens top-3 accuracy against the final top-1 is about 40% in middle layers and rises from layer 21 onward — [arXiv 2603.11947](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11947) (snippet). A LessWrong post finds the logit lens weak in the middle layers (L18 to 21) of Qwen3.5-4B — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Qe3jdphWCg9kny3e5) (snippet, small n)

### Inferences
- Since flip rate ≈ TV and greedy acceptance tracks TV, the drafter's reconstruction quality should be reported as teacher-forced TV and top-1 agreement against the target's own logits, not as MSE or KL alone.
- Layer 18 (half depth) of Qwen3-8B will not decode usefully through the raw LM head. Layers 24 and 30 likely partially decode for function words but not for content tokens. Only the final state should be decoded through the frozen head, which matches the proposed design. Intermediate-layer latents are conditioning signals, not decodable states.
- Near-ties set the floor. Any positions with tiny margins will flip under almost any reconstruction error. Those flips are also less costly under sampling-based verification, because the target distribution there is spread out.

### Gaps
- I found no published margin distribution (fraction of positions with top-1/top-2 logit gap below x) for Qwen3-8B or any comparable model. It must be measured; it costs one forward pass.
- The full text of 2610.00694 (margin analysis, per-model numbers) could not be read; the PDF was not parseable.

## Q3. Smoothness and manifold structure: intrinsic dimension, plateaus, off-manifold states, steering and patching robustness

### Takeaway
Real residual activations sit on a low-dimensional, curved manifold whose intrinsic dimension peaks in the middle layers. Real activations sit at the center of "plateaus": the output barely moves until the perturbation is large (about 40° rotation in GPT-2 layer 1). But off-manifold points (random vectors, synthetic SAE combinations) lack plateaus, and learned reconstruction errors are 2 to 4.5x more harmful than random errors of equal norm. Smoothness around real states is real. A learned decoder's errors are not random, though, so isotropic-noise tests overestimate tolerance.

### Cited Findings
- "Emergence of a High-Dimensional Abstraction Phase in Language Transformers", arXiv 2405.15471 (ICLR 2025). Across five LMs, intrinsic dimension (GRIDE estimator) peaks in middle layers. The peak marks the first full linguistic abstraction, is reduced on random text, and is absent in untrained models — [arXiv 2405.15471](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15471v3)
- Local intrinsic dimension follows a "hunchback": it rises from bottom to middle layers, then falls toward the top — [arXiv 2402.18048](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18048) (snippet). ID peaks in early-to-middle layers and correlates with next-token uncertainty — [arXiv 2501.10573](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10573) (snippet). In MCQA, early layers are low-dimensional, middle layers expand, and late layers compress — [arXiv 2511.20315](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20315) (snippet)
- DiHAL, "Where Should Diffusion Enter a Language Model?", arXiv 2605.14368 (Kong, Lee, Jo), on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B. It scores layers by local curvature, global stiffness and effective rank, and finds that "bridgeability is shallow". The best diffusion interface for Qwen3-8B is layer 1 or 2, and the worst is layer 35. Fully trained, Qwen3-8B with a diffusion bridge at layer 2 reaches NLL 4.97, PPL 144.03, teacher KL 0.53 (layer 35: PPL 186.79, KL 1.46), against the original PPL 13.55 (NLL 2.61). That is 10.63x worse PPL for Qwen. In a reduced-budget Llama comparison, latent diffusion got hidden MSE 0.0067 yet still teacher KL 5.74 and PPL 2,081. Other bridges (MLP, CVAE, flow matching, hidden-space DDPM) were all worse, and hidden-space DDPM was worst (KL 11.2) — [arXiv 2605.14368](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14368v2)
- Heimersheim & Mendel (Apollo), "Activation plateaus & sensitive directions in GPT2" (July 2024). GPT-2 small, perturbation at blocks.1 resid_pre. For real activations, output KL barely changes until about 40° of slerp rotation toward another direction. Random Gaussian base activations show no plateau (KL rises immediately). Covariance-matched random directions are more sensitive than isotropic ones. The "real directions are more sensitive" result was later downgraded by the authors (Lee & Heimersheim, Sept 2024) as possibly a baseline artifact — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LajDyGyiyX8DNNsuF/interim-research-report-activation-plateaus-and-sensitive-1)
- Giglemiani, Petrova, Heimersheim, arXiv 2409.15019. Synthetic activations built from SAE latents do not have plateaus the way real activations do, so arbitrary combinations of features do not look like real activations — [arXiv 2409.15019](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15019v2). Related stable-regions work: [arXiv 2409.17113](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.17113v1)
- Gurnee, "SAE reconstruction errors are (empirically) pathological" (AF, Mar 2024), GPT-2 small residual SAEs (32x). Substituting the SAE reconstruction gives 2.2 to 4.5x higher KL and 2 to 4x higher CE-loss increase than a random perturbation of the same L2 error norm. Rescaling the original to the SAE's norm gives almost 0 KL, so the damage comes from direction, not norm. For attention-out SAEs the gap is smaller (0.9 to 1.7x) — [Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/rZPiuFxESMxCDHe4B/sae-reconstruction-errors-are-empir)

### Inferences
- A drafter whose latent space covers the real activation manifold should benefit from plateaus. Small errors along the manifold are tolerated. Errors that leave the manifold (which learned decoders and diffusion samplers produce) are disproportionately harmful.
- Gurnee's direction-vs-norm result, together with RMSNorm scale invariance, says the final-layer loss should weight direction (cosine) heavily. Norm errors at the final layer are nearly free.
- DiHAL shows that, even with a direct Qwen3-8B diffusion experiment, tiny hidden-space MSE did not give low KL. Diffusing raw hidden states (hidden DDPM) was the worst option, and a latent (autoencoded) space did best. This supports autoencoding before diffusing. It also warns that MSE is not a sufficient training or selection metric, and that late layers (35) were the hardest interface in their setting. Their task (replacing lower layers) differs from drafting final states, so the layer ranking may not transfer.
- Steering and patching: I did not gather quantitative steering-robustness numbers this session. The plateau results are the most relevant robustness evidence found.

### Gaps
- No intrinsic-dimension-by-layer measurement specific to Qwen3-8B was found.
- I found no direct measurement of whether random (Gaussian) final-layer states decode to "garbage" tokens in Qwen. Under RMSNorm and a tied or untied head, a random direction decodes to whatever token row has the largest projection, likely a fixed set of high-norm token embeddings [UNVERIFIED inference].
- Activation-steering robustness numbers (e.g. the coefficient at which fluency degrades) were not collected.

## Q4. Reconstruction-error tolerance when compressing hidden states (SAE loss penalties) and implications for the drafter

### Takeaway
Even very good SAEs cost a lot of next-token loss when spliced in. OpenAI's 16M-latent GPT-4 SAE gives a loss equivalent to a model trained with about 10% of GPT-4's pretraining compute. Errors that look small by normalized MSE are large in loss terms. Thresholds should therefore be set in output space (top-1 agreement, TV, KL against the target), not hidden-state MSE.

### Cited Findings
- Gao et al., arXiv 2406.04093. Splicing the 16M-latent SAE into GPT-4 (layer 5/6 deep) gives "a language modeling loss corresponding to 10% of the pretraining compute of GPT-4". Normalized MSE is defined as MSE divided by the MSE of predicting the mean activation — [arXiv 2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04093v1)
- Low-rank adapters on the model reduce the SAE-induced loss gap by 30 to 55% (Gemma Scope / Gemma-2-2B setting). Inserting reconstructions leaves loss well above the original — [arXiv 2501.19406](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19406v2)
- A short end-to-end (KL-to-original) finetune of SAEs improves the CE penalty, with mixed effects elsewhere — [arXiv 2503.17272](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17272) (snippet)
- The nonlinear part of SAE error contributes to the downstream loss increase in proportion to its size — [arXiv 2410.14670](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14670v2) (snippet)
- [UNVERIFIED, from memory] EAGLE (arXiv 2401.15077) trains its feature-level drafter with a smooth-L1 regression loss on the target's second-to-top features plus a cross-entropy loss through the frozen LM head. EAGLE-3 (arXiv 2503.01840) dropped feature regression, reporting that it limited scaling. Paper identity confirmed — [arXiv 2401.15077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15077); loss details not confirmed this session.

### Inferences (concrete implications for the drafter)

**Normalization to use**
1. Measure Qwen3-8B's massive-activation dims and carrier tokens at layers 18/24/30/36 first. Treat those (dim, position-type) entries as constants (Sun: mean-substitution is lossless) and exclude them from loss and from standardization statistics. Exclude position 0 / BOS entirely.
2. For layers 18/24/30, apply per-dimension standardization (mean and std from about 1M tokens, outliers excluded), following Timkey. Alternatively use Gao-style mean-subtraction plus unit-norm with a separately predicted log-norm. Unit-norm alone leaves rogue dims dominant, so pair it with per-dim scaling.
3. For the final layer, target RMSNorm_final(h) (with gain g applied) or the direction h/‖h‖. Use a cosine or direction loss plus a KL/CE loss through the frozen LM head against the target's logits. That loss is the one aligned with acceptance. Do not rely on MSE alone (DiHAL: MSE 0.0067 still gave KL 5.7; Gurnee: direction errors dominate).
4. Diffuse in the autoencoder's latent, not raw hidden space (DiHAL: hidden-space DDPM was the worst bridge).

**Reconstruction thresholds (proposed, not from literature)**
- The primary metric is teacher-forced top-1 agreement of LM_head(decode(encode(h_final))) with LM_head(h_final). Because greedy acceptance ≈ 1 − TV-flip rate (2610.00694), an autoencoder round-trip should reach at least 98 to 99% top-1 agreement and mean TV ≤ 0.02. Otherwise it caps acceptance before the diffusion model contributes any error. This is a design target, not a published number.
- Mean KL(target ‖ reconstruction) should be well under 0.1 nats. Reference points: DiHAL's KL 0.53 on Qwen3-8B was a 10.6x PPL blowup, and SAE splices that cost about 10% of pretraining compute are already unacceptable for a drafter.
- Report agreement stratified by target margin bucket, since flips concentrate on low-margin positions.

**Cheap noise-sensitivity test (about 1 GPU-hour)**
1. Run Qwen3-8B on about 50k tokens of target-domain text. Cache pre-final-norm h_36 plus h_18/24/30 and the target logits.
2. Analytic final-layer flip radius. For each position, with top-1 token i and runner-up j, compute the minimal perturbation that flips the argmax in the normalized space: r = (z_i − z_j) / ‖(W_i − W_j) ⊙ g‖. Express it relative to ‖RMSNorm(h)‖. Its distribution is the exact "how far off can a state be" answer for a linear readout. Report the quantiles and the fraction with r below 1%, 5% and 10% of the norm (the near-tie frequency).
3. Empirical curves. Add perturbations at relative norm ε ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40}% of four kinds: (a) isotropic Gaussian, (b) covariance-matched Gaussian (from cached stats), (c) "real-other" direction toward another position's state, (d) slerp rotation θ ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20, 40}°. Measure top-1 flip rate, TV and KL. Plot them against ε, also stratified by margin.
4. Mid-layer propagation. Repeat step 3 at layers 18/24/30, running the remaining layers, to see whether plateaus (Heimersheim) exist in Qwen3-8B and how the angle tolerance changes with depth.
5. Learned-error check. Pass states through a small trained autoencoder and compare its flip rate with the same-norm random baseline (the Gurnee ratio). A ratio well above 1 means isotropic tests overestimate tolerance, so thresholds must be set from learned-error measurements.

### Gaps

- No literature number ties hidden-state reconstruction cosine or NMSE to top-1 agreement for any model. The thresholds above are proposals to be validated by the test in steps 1 to 5.
- EAGLE and EAGLE-3 loss details are unverified, as stated above.
