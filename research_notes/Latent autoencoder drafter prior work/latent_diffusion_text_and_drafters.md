# Latent diffusion / flow matching for text, and diffusion-based drafters for speculative decoding

Scope: prior work for a proposed drafter that (1) autoencodes a frozen Qwen3-8B target's future hidden states (next k tokens) into a latent, (2) predicts/samples that latent with diffusion or flow, (3) decodes through the target's frozen LM head, and (4) builds a draft tree from several sampled latents, verified losslessly.

Stage tags used below: [AE] = autoencoder over hidden states/text, [PRED] = diffusion/flow predictor in continuous space, [TREE] = tree from multiple samples, [HEAD] = frozen target LM head reuse.

## (a) Latent diffusion / flow matching over continuous text latents or LLM hidden states

### Takeaway
Latent text diffusion is well established (PLANNER, LD4LG, Large Concept Models, and a 2026 wave of flow-matching LMs such as FLM/FMLM, LangFlow, TextLDM, ELF), but these are standalone generators over latents from a separately trained text autoencoder or sentence encoder (SONAR), not over a frozen decoder-only LLM's hidden states, and none is used as a lossless drafter. They cover the [AE] and [PRED] stages conceptually but not the conditioning-on-target or verification parts.

### Cited Findings
- PLANNER (Zhang, Gu, Wu, Zhai, Susskind, Jaitly; Apple; NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2306.02531): latent diffusion "planning" module generates a fixed number of continuous semantic tokens encoding a paragraph, coarse to fine; an autoregressive decoder maps them to text. Claims less repetitive, more diverse output than AR and text-diffusion baselines at similar fluency. Authors list the AR decoder as a limitation. Code at github.com/apple/ml-planner. Closeness: [AE]+[PRED], paragraph-level, own autoencoder, no frozen LLM target. — [arXiv 2306.02531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02531v3); [NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/fdba5e0a9b57fce03e89cc0cad0a24e9-Abstract.html); [Apple ML research](https://pr-mlr-shield-prod.apple.com/research/planner)
- LD4LG, "Latent Diffusion for Language Generation" (Lovelace, Kishore, Wan, Shekhtman, Weinberger; arXiv 2212.09462, listed as NeurIPS 2023): uses pretrained encoder-decoder LMs to learn a language autoencoder, trains continuous diffusion in that latent space, and decodes sampled latents with the pretrained decoder. Claims "significantly more effective than previous diffusion language models" for unconditional, class-conditional, and seq2seq generation. Abstract does not give latent dims or whether encoder/decoder are frozen. Closeness: [AE]+[PRED] with pretrained-LM-derived latents (encoder-decoder, not decoder-only hidden states). — [arXiv 2212.09462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09462)
- Large Concept Models (Meta LCM team, Barrault, Duquenne, Elbayad, ... Schwenk; arXiv 2412.08821, Dec 2024): autoregressive sentence prediction in the SONAR sentence-embedding space (200 languages, text and speech). Explored MSE regression, diffusion-based variants, and quantized-SONAR models. 1.6B models trained on about 1.3T tokens; one architecture scaled to 7B on about 2.7T tokens. Evaluated on summarization and a new "summary expansion" task; claims zero-shot multilingual generalization beating same-size LLMs. Closeness: [PRED] via diffusion over a frozen pretrained embedding space, at sentence granularity rather than per-token hidden states. — [arXiv 2412.08821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08821)
- Flow Map Language Models (arXiv 2602.16813, 2026): a continuous flow LM (FLM) matching state-of-the-art discrete diffusion baselines on LM1B and OpenWebText, distilled into a flow map LM (FMLM) whose one-step generation beats the 8-step quality of recent few-step discrete diffusion LMs. This is the base technique FlashMap later turns into a drafter. Closeness: [PRED], few-step continuous flow; space is one-hot/embedding, not hidden states. — [arXiv 2602.16813](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16813)
- LangFlow (arXiv 2604.11748; NeurIPS 2026 poster): connects embedding-space diffusion LMs to flow matching with a learnable noise scheduler; title claims continuous diffusion rivals discrete in language modeling. — [arXiv 2604.11748](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11748v2); [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/150769)
- TextLDM (arXiv 2605.07748): trains a TextVAE, freezes the encoder, and trains a Diffusion Transformer with flow matching in that latent space; supports unconditional passage generation. Closeness: [AE]+[PRED] pattern closest to the proposed pipeline, but standalone LM, own VAE. — [arXiv 2605.07748](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07748)
- ELF: Embedded Language Flows (arXiv 2605.10938): flow matching on continuous token embeddings with x-prediction (predict clean embeddings, not velocity). — [arXiv 2605.10938](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10938)
- Draft-conditioned latent refinement technical report (arXiv 2605.15557): reported as a negative diagnostic result, concluding "latent geometry alone is not enough." Relevant as a cautionary data point for latent-space refinement. — [arXiv 2605.15557](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15557) (summary from search snippet only, not fully read)
- "The Generative Gap Between Speculative Drafter and Continuous Diffusion Language Model" (Chen, Jiang, Cheng, Luo, Hu; arXiv 2610.40847): controlled from-scratch comparison on unconditional OpenWebText generation of LangFlow (continuous diffusion LM) vs DSpark drafters (Markov-head and recurrent-head variants) used standalone, with no target features or verification. LangFlow beats both DSpark variants on GenPPL and MAUVE at length 128 and 1024; DSpark-RNN leads at length 16; more LangFlow steps improve quality. Authors conclude iterative full-sequence refinement is promising beyond recurrent head memory. — [arXiv 2610.40847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40847v1)
- A 2026 paper's related work cites flow matching in continuous latents learned by text autoencoders (Jiang et al., 2026). — [arXiv 2610.19871](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19871)

### Inferences
- The standard recipe is: train (or reuse) a text autoencoder, freeze it, train diffusion/flow in its latent. The proposal swaps the text autoencoder for an autoencoder over a frozen LLM's own future hidden states with the frozen LM head as part of the decoder. None of the latent-diffusion LM papers above uses decoder-only LLM hidden states as the latent space.
- 2026 flow LMs (FMLM, LangFlow) show few-step or one-step continuous generation can match discrete diffusion quality, which supports the feasibility of a 1-2 step latent predictor for drafting.

### Gaps
- CDCD (Dieleman et al., continuous diffusion for categorical data) and Diffusion-LM (Li et al. 2022) were not fetched this session. Details for them are unverified here.
- No numbers were extracted for LD4LG, PLANNER, LangFlow, TextLDM, or ELF (abstract-level only). Model sizes for most 2026 flow LMs are not recorded.
- No paper found that trains diffusion/flow over a frozen decoder-only LLM's hidden states as a standalone generator (as distinct from drafting, below).

## (b) Diffusion or block-parallel drafters for speculative decoding

### Takeaway
Diffusion drafting is now a dominant direction. Pretrained-dLLM drafters (SpecDiff, DiffuSpec, SpecDiff-2) work training-free or with alignment, while target-conditioned block drafters (DFlash, then DDTree, DFlow, MFD) reach about 5-7x lossless speedup on Qwen3-8B. Two late-2026 papers already use continuous flow conditioned on target hidden states with a frozen LM head: FlashMap (flow over one-hot blocks, multi-noise-sample tree) and ChainFlow (VAE over target hidden states plus 1-2 step latent flow, decoded through the frozen LM head).

### Cited Findings
Discrete / masked diffusion drafters
- Speculative Diffusion Decoding, SpecDiff (Christopher, Bartoldson, Ben-Nun, Cardei, Kailkhura, Fioretto; NAACL 2025): pretrained (unaligned) discrete diffusion LM as drafter for an AR verifier. Math500 greedy: 3.19x (Qwen2.5-72B) and 2.51x (Qwen2.5-14B), as reported in the SpecDiff-2 paper. Does not use target hidden states. — [SpecDiff-2 paper, arXiv 2511.00606](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00606v2) (SpecDiff arXiv ID not confirmed in fetched text; commonly cited as 2408.05636, unverified)
- SpecDiff-2 (Sandler, Christopher, Hartvigsen, Fioretto; arXiv 2511.00606): drafters DiffuCoder-7B and DiffuLLaMA-7B, verifiers Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and LLaMA-2-70B-chat (also 14B/13B). Train-time streak-distillation plus test-time self-selection acceptance. Average 4.22x across settings, up to 5.51x (Qwen2.5-72B, HumanEval, T=1). Acceptance 6.47 tokens/draft on Qwen2.5-72B Math-500 T=0 vs EAGLE-2 4.69. About 45% over SpecDiff on Math500. Lossless. No target hidden states. — [arXiv 2511.00606](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00606v2)
- DiffuSpec (arXiv 2510.02358; Findings of ACL 2026): training-free; pretrained diffusion LM drafts a multi-token block in one pass, with a causal-consistency component and adaptive draft length. Up to 3x wall-clock speedup. Drop-in, no target changes. — [arXiv 2510.02358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02358v1); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1048/)
- LibraSpec (arXiv 2608.08721): dynamic diffusion-based speculative decoding via marginal-gain-driven optimization. Not read beyond title. — [arXiv 2608.08721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08721)

Target-conditioned block drafters
- DFlash (Jian Chen, Yesheng Liang, Zhijian Liu; z-lab; arXiv 2602.06036; ICML 2026): 5-layer block diffusion drafter (8 for Qwen3-Coder), block size 16 (10 for LLaMA-3.1). Fuses hidden features from 5 target layers (uniform between layer 2 and third-to-last) and injects them into K/V of every draft layer. Shares and freezes target embedding and LM head. Qwen3-8B, thinking off, T=0, budget 16: average 4.86x / tau 6.49 (e.g. MATH-500 6.08x / 7.87, GSM8K 5.15x / 6.54, MT-Bench 2.75x / 4.24) vs EAGLE-3(16) 1.76x / 2.96 and EAGLE-3(60) 2.02x / 3.40. T=1: average 4.03x / 5.48. Reasoning mode about 4.2-4.6x at T=0. Lossless. Verifies a single trajectory. Closeness: [HEAD] + target-hidden-state conditioning, discrete per-slot factorized prediction. — [arXiv 2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/chen26bv.html)
- DDTree (Ringel, Romano; arXiv 2604.12989): builds a draft tree from DFlash's per-position distributions with a best-first heap under a node budget ({16..1024}), verified in one pass with ancestor-only attention. Qwen3-8B T=0, DFlash -> DFlash+DDTree: MATH-500 5.56x/7.79 -> 7.52x/10.73; GSM8K 4.78x/6.57 -> 6.75x/9.54; HumanEval 4.84x/6.61 -> 6.90x/9.67; MT-Bench 2.56x/4.28 -> 4.10x/6.58; Alpaca 2.07x/3.12 -> 3.36x/5.09. Improves all 60 settings (Qwen3-4B, 8B, Coder-30B-A3B). Note DFlash baseline numbers differ from the DFlash paper (different setup). Closeness: [TREE] from factorized token marginals, not from multiple sampled latents. — [arXiv 2604.12989](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12989)
- Multi-Future Drafting, MFD (Huang, Kang, Yang, Yang, Zhang; arXiv 2611.13017, Nov 2026): argues factorized trees (DFlash+DDTree) splice futures into "chimeras", 27-33% of a 64-node tree. Uses K learned mode embeddings (a latent variable) so the drafter emits K coherent blocks in one pass, then a mixture DDTree. Up to 7.8x on Qwen models 4B-27B, 5-8% over best single-mode tree, 9-16% over released DARTree on Qwen3-4B/8B. Lossless. Closeness: [TREE] from multiple coherent futures via a latent mode variable, the nearest motivation to "several sampled latents", but modes are a learned discrete set of embeddings, not continuous samples. — [arXiv 2611.13017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13017)
- DFlow (arXiv 2609.06498): carries verifier hidden states from rejected suffixes into later block-diffusion drafting rounds. Numbers not extracted. — [arXiv 2609.06498](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06498)
- DART (Liu et al., Nanjing Univ./Alibaba; arXiv 2601.19278): one custom decoder layer reads target hidden states and predicts logits for multiple future masked positions in one pass, plus 3-gram-guided tree pruning. T=0 mean: Qwen3-8B 2.71x / tau 3.61 vs EAGLE-3 2.20x / 3.72; range 2.03x-3.44x; about 30% over EAGLE-3. Lossless. Gains shrink at larger batch (near 1x vs EAGLE-3 at batch >=16 on Qwen3-8B). — [arXiv 2601.19278](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19278v1)
- Other related titles found but not read: TreeFlash (arXiv 2606.03819), H-Spec (arXiv 2609.24197). — [TreeFlash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03819); [H-Spec](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24197)

Continuous flow drafters conditioned on target hidden states
- FlashMap (Shen, Zhou, Lee, May, Ho; Polymathic AI/Princeton/USC ISI; arXiv 2610.24365, Oct 2026): rectified flow from Gaussian noise to the one-hot encoding of a K=16 block, x-prediction with softmax trained by cross-entropy, distilled to a two-time flow map for 1 or 2 NFE. Conditional DiT, 6 blocks, dim 2048, about 500M trainable params on Qwen3-8B (DFlash about 1049M, EAGLE-3 302M). Conditioned on target layers 1, 9, 17, 25, 33 plus anchor token; target embedding and LM head copied and frozen. Lossless (Leviathan rejection rule). Qwen3-8B T=0 speedup, 1 step: GSM8K 6.14x, MATH 5.89x, HumanEval 4.73x, MT-Bench 2.73x vs DFlash 5.77x/5.59x/4.50x/2.48x (retrained on same data). Acceptance (2 steps, T=0): GSM8K 8.31, MATH 7.69 vs DFlash 7.43, 7.09. About 7.4% average speedup over DFlash. Appendix D: four independent noise draws merged into a 16-token tree raised speedup 4-24% (about 11% avg) at 1 step. Qwen3-4B results mixed. Only concurrency 1, no DDTree baseline. Closeness: very close on [PRED] (continuous flow, few-step, target-conditioned, Qwen3-8B, frozen [HEAD]) and [TREE] (multi-sample tree from independent noise draws). Differs in that the flow space is one-hot token blocks, not a learned latent of hidden states (no [AE]). — [arXiv 2610.24365](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24365)
- ChainFlow (Aktas, Erdem, Zeer, Kesgin, Yuce, Amasyali; arXiv 2611.08669, Nov 2026): "first flow-matching drafter conditioned on the target's hidden states." VAE (two-layer bidirectional transformer encoder/decoder over windows up to 16 states) compresses the target's final hidden states; latent dim 640 for Qwen3.5-4B (hidden 2560), 1024 for 9B and 27B; KL beta 3e-5; VAE fine-tuned jointly with drafter (recon weight 0.5); posterior mean used. Decoded latents are projected by the target's frozen lm_head. Drafter 529M / 1.16B / 1.64B; 2 Euler steps in training, 1 at serving; lightweight feedback heads restore inter-token dependencies. Tree built by token beam search (top candidates per depth, shortlist M=8192), 8x4 = 32 nodes; no mention of sampling multiple latents. Qwen3.5-4B batch 1: tree tau 2.78, 1.87x vs DFlash 2.35 / 1.69x and EAGLE-3 2.49 / 1.35x; chain-only 2.01 / 1.23x. Tree speedups 1.87x / 1.78x / 2.12x at 4B / 9B / 27B. Abstract: beats EAGLE-3 by 39% and DFlash by 11% in vLLM. Tree verification greedy-only; 4-13% of prompts differ from plain greedy due to fp16 near-ties, authors say this does not establish bitwise equivalence. — [arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08669)

### Inferences
- On Qwen3-8B the bar for a new drafter is DFlash (about 4.9x avg T=0 in its paper) and DFlash+DDTree (about 6.4-7.5x on reasoning tasks at T=0), with FlashMap about 6x on GSM8K. MFD reports up to 7.8x. A latent drafter needs to clear these, not just EAGLE-3.
- ChainFlow's absolute speedups (about 1.7-2.1x on Qwen3.5) are much lower than DFlash numbers on Qwen3, likely due to different target family, engine (vLLM), and settings. Cross-paper comparison is unsafe.
- ChainFlow's 4-13% output mismatch shows that decoding hidden-state latents in fp16 and tree verification in a patched engine can break exactness. A proposal claiming losslessness should verify bitwise equivalence explicitly.

### Gaps
- DDTree, DFlow, MFD temperature>0 numbers, and MFD acceptance lengths were not extracted.
- FlashMap's draft tree appendix gives only percentage gains, not absolute tau for the tree.
- No GitHub README or HF model card was checked this session (z-lab DFlash checkpoints, DDTree, ChainFlow code are referenced but not read).
- EAGLE/EAGLE-2/EAGLE-3 (hidden-state feature regression with frozen LM head) were only confirmed via an aggregator blog, not primary sources in this session.

## (c) Has anyone drafted in a continuous latent space of the target's hidden states with diffusion/flow, or sampled multiple latents to form a draft tree?

### Takeaway
Yes, partly. ChainFlow (Nov 2026) does the [AE]+[PRED]+[HEAD] core of the proposal: a VAE over the target's final hidden states, a 1-2 step flow drafter conditioned on target hidden states, and decoding through the frozen LM head, but it builds its tree by token beam search, not from multiple latent samples, and is greedy-only with measured non-exactness. FlashMap (Oct 2026) does the multi-sample tree from independent noise draws on Qwen3-8B, but flows over one-hot tokens rather than a hidden-state latent. No paper found combines both: sampling several continuous hidden-state latents and merging their LM-head decodings into a lossless tree.

### Cited Findings
- ChainFlow compresses the target's final hidden states with a VAE, runs a few-step flow over that latent, and projects decoded latents through the target's frozen lm_head. — [arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)
- ChainFlow's tree uses beam search over top token candidates per depth, and the fetched text does not mention sampling multiple latents. Its posterior mean (deterministic) encoding is used. — [arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)
- ChainFlow claims "No prior work uses a flow-matching transport as an autoregressive drafter, or conditions one on the target's hidden states" (authors' claim; FlashMap, posted earlier in Oct 2026, conditions a flow drafter on target hidden states, so the claim is contestable). — [ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669); [FlashMap](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24365)
- FlashMap drafts several blocks from independent noise draws and verifies them as a merged tree (4 samples, 16 nodes), gaining about 11% average speedup. Because one noise draw determines the whole block, samples are internally coherent, unlike DFlash's per-slot factorization. — [arXiv 2610.24365](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24365)
- MFD reaches multiple coherent futures through K learned mode embeddings (a latent variable) and a mixture tree, citing chimera branches as the failure of factorized trees. — [arXiv 2611.13017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13017)
- A search for drafters sampling continuous latent vectors found no further sources beyond these. — [search result summary incl. Multi-Future Drafting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13017)

### Inferences
- Stage coverage vs the proposal: [AE] over target hidden states = ChainFlow (final layer, VAE, latent 640-1024). [PRED] continuous few-step flow conditioned on target = ChainFlow, FlashMap. [HEAD] frozen LM head decode = ChainFlow, FlashMap, DFlash. [TREE] from multiple stochastic samples = FlashMap (noise draws), MFD (discrete modes); from token marginals = DDTree, ChainFlow, DART.
- The remaining novelty for the proposal is narrow: stochastic multi-latent sampling (rather than posterior mean plus token beam search) to form a tree of coherent futures in hidden-state latent space, on Qwen3-8B, with a verified exactly-lossless implementation (including temperature>0). The proposal should benchmark directly against ChainFlow, FlashMap (with its multi-sample tree), DFlash+DDTree, and MFD on Qwen3-8B.
- FlashMap and MFD evidence suggests that coherent multi-sample trees beat factorized trees at matched budget by roughly 5-11%, which bounds the plausible gain from the multi-latent tree component.

### Gaps
- Whether ChainFlow tried stochastic latent sampling in ablations is unknown (only HTML main text read).
- No source found for a drafter that predicts multiple intermediate-layer future hidden states (rather than final-layer) as the latent.
- Code availability and licensing of ChainFlow and FlashMap not verified.
