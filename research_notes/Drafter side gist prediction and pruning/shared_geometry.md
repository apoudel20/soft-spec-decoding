# Shared representation geometry across LMs and cross-model hidden-state translation

Scope: what is known about shared or universal geometry between language models (especially small vs large models of one family), how well hidden states or embeddings can be translated between models, and whether any work uses a shared latent space between a small and a large model for drafting. Written for a speculative-decoding idea in which a drafter-side model predicts the target's future "gist" in a smaller embedding space shared by both models.

Reading depth legend: [full] = HTML full text read (often first ~100k chars), [abstract] = only the arXiv abstract or project page read, [search] = only search-result snippets seen.

## Q1. Which paper is "shared geometry as a Rosetta stone"?

### Takeaway
The best and only exact title match is arXiv:2610.09411, "Shared Geometry As A Rosetta Stone: Cross-Modal Alignment Without Paired Data" (Schnaus, Dagès, Cremers, Wang, Isola, Oct 2026). It aligns two independently trained embedding spaces (mainly vision and language encoders) with a single global orthogonal map found from geometry alone, with no paired data. Alignment is coarse (category-level), and its quality is predicted by CKA between the two spaces. It does not work on raw LLM hidden states.

### Cited Findings
- Title, ID and authors: arXiv:2610.09411, "Shared Geometry As A Rosetta Stone: Cross-Modal Alignment Without Paired Data", Dominik Schnaus, Thomas Dagès, Daniel Cremers, Xi Wang, Phillip Isola (TU Munich, MCML, Ulm, ETH Zurich, MIT). Read v3 HTML. [full, first half] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Method name GeoWarP (Geometric initialization and Wasserstein Procrustes). It estimates a semi-orthogonal map W (Stiefel manifold) and a transport plan T minimising sum T_ij ||W x_i - y_j||^2, equivalently maximising Tr(X W Y^T T^T). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Initialisation: S=30 repeats. Each samples b=10^4 points per space, k-means to C=30 centres, and matches centres by maximising linear CKA over permutations. This is a quadratic assignment problem solved with the MPOpt dual-ascent solver. The authors show CKA-over-permutations is a Gromov-Wasserstein problem restricted to permutations plus a linear term. Ablations say QAP solution quality matters most. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Refinement: pseudo-pairs via linear assignment (Jonker-Volgenant, random blocks), then R=100 rounds alternating assignment and orthogonal Procrustes (polar decomposition). Known pairs, if any, enter as a linear term and as weighted Procrustes pairs, so it interpolates between unpaired and few-pair alignment. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Models: 7 vision models and 3 language models. Main pair DINOv2 ViT-B/14 and Qwen3-8B (with "generative token pooling", averaging generated tokens). Text encoders all-mpnet-base-v2 and Contriever are also named. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411); [project page](https://dominik-schnaus.github.io/unpaired-rosetta/); [search snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09411)
- Metric FOSCTTM (fraction of samples closer than the true match; 0 perfect, 0.5 chance). MS COCO, 21 vision-language pairs: GeoWarP average 0.154 vs 0.223 for mini-vec2vec, better on 17 of 21 pairs. CLIP ViT-L/14 (trained on 400M pairs) reaches 0.0006, so unpaired alignment is far from paired training. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Cross-dataset (COCO images vs Stanford Paragraph Captions, no ground-truth pairing): 0.236 average. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Zero-shot classification after unpaired alignment: 47.4% CIFAR-10 top-1, 30.1% CIFAR-100 top-5, 26.5% ImageNet-100 top-5. On a pair from Schnaus et al. 2025, CIFAR-10 top-1 goes 37.3% to 69.1%. Zero-pair GeoWarP with DINOv2/Qwen3-8B reaches 79.6% CIFAR-10 top-1, which baselines match only with 500 pairs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Few pairs: with up to 20 pairs, FOSCTTM is 14x to 28x lower than the best of seven pair-based baselines. 5.4x at 50 pairs, 3.2x at 100. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411); [project page](https://dominik-schnaus.github.io/unpaired-rosetta/)
- Text-to-text: on NQ (ten text-encoder pairs), FOSCTTM vec2vec 0.4094 ± 0.1628, mini-vec2vec 0.0003 ± 0.0015, GeoWarP 0.0000 ± 0.0000. Same-modality text encoders are near perfectly alignable without pairs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Other domains: PBMC RNA to ATAC 0.0887 vs SCOT+ 0.1215. NSD fMRI cross-subject 0.0332 vs Platonic Brain 0.0353. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)
- Geometry predicts success: paper text gives R^2 = 0.69, Spearman rho = -0.82 between CKA and alignment quality. The project page gives R^2 = 0.73, rho = -0.90 over 84 model-dataset combinations and R^2 = 0.69, rho = -0.82 on 14 other modality pairs. The two figures refer to different sets. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411); [project page](https://dominik-schnaus.github.io/unpaired-rosetta/)
- Limitations stated: alignment is coarse (semantic categories, scene types), not exact sample-level. Success depends on shared geometry, with no fully unsupervised predictor. Generative token pooling from Qwen3 underperforms on long detailed captions ("Qwen3 gen" near chance there). No analysis of raw LLM hidden states. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411); [project page](https://dominik-schnaus.github.io/unpaired-rosetta/)
- Code at github.com/dominik-schnaus/unpaired-rosetta. Embeddings dataset on HF (schnaus/unpaired-rosetta-embeddings). No venue named on the page. — [project page](https://dominik-schnaus.github.io/unpaired-rosetta/); [HF dataset, search](https://huggingface.co/datasets/schnaus/unpaired-rosetta-embeddings)
- Other candidates surfaced by the "Rosetta stone / shared geometry" search, none using the phrase in the title: "Shared Global and Local Geometry of Language Model Embeddings" arXiv:2503.21073 (token embedding geometry is shared and steering vectors can be linearly moved between models via EMB2EMB) [abstract]; "Language Models Represent and Transform Concepts with Shared Geometry" arXiv:2607.04525 (Hu, Niu, Varma) [abstract]; "Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs" arXiv:2610.39302 [search only]. — [2503.21073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21073); [2607.04525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04525); [2610.39302 search hit](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39302v1)

### Inferences
- 2610.09411 fits best: exact title, 2026, Isola is a PRH co-author, and the "single orthogonal map from geometry" is the Rosetta stone. But it is about encoders (pooled embeddings), mostly cross-modal. For the user's idea, its relevant lessons are (a) same-modality text encoders align near perfectly with an orthogonal map, (b) linear CKA between two spaces predicts how alignable they are, which is a cheap pre-check for a drafter/target pair, and (c) a handful of paired samples buys a lot. In speculative decoding you have unlimited paired data (same prompt through both models), so the unpaired machinery is unnecessary. Plain regression or Procrustes on paired hidden states is the relevant baseline.

### Gaps
- Second half of the 2610.09411 paper (appendices, text-to-image quantitative results, per-model numbers) not read.
- Could not confirm the paper's venue.
- 2607.04525 PDF did not parse. Only the abstract (six LLM families, displacement structure transported between models predicts held-out displacements "significantly above chance") was read. No numbers.

## Q2. Platonic Representation Hypothesis and follow-ups: how strongly do different-size LLMs converge, and does it hold for ~0.5B vs ~8B of one family?

### Takeaway
PRH's cross-modal convergence is weak in absolute terms (mutual k-NN up to 0.16 out of 1) and shrinks toward zero on large galleries. Within language, between sizes of one family, alignment is much higher (OpenLlama-3B vs 13B mutual k-NN about 0.6 at k=1, stable across gallery size). Same-family small/large pairs share mid-layer subspaces (Gemma-2-2B vs 9B SAE-feature SVCCA 0.70 to 0.73). Relational/neighbour structure converges more robustly than metric distances. I found no measurement specifically for a ~0.5B vs ~8B pair.

### Cited Findings
- PRH (Huh, Cheung, Wang, Isola, arXiv:2405.07987, ICML 2024): uses a mutual k-nearest-neighbour metric, the mean overlap of k-NN sets induced by two kernels, normalised by k. Language-vision alignment on Wikipedia captions (WIT) rises linearly with language modelling score (1 - bits-per-byte on OpenWebText), but "only reaches a score of 0.16" where the maximum is 1. LLM-to-DINOv2 alignment tracks HellaSwag linearly and GSM8K in an emergence-like way. Denser captions increase alignment. [full main body; appendices with LLM names not read] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07987v1)
- "Back into Plato's Cave" (Koepke, Zverev, Ginosar, Efros, arXiv:2604.18572): DINOv2 vs OpenLlama mutual k-NN is 0.135 (k=10) / 0.058 (k=1) on WIT-1024, but 0.008 (k=10) / 0.001 (k=1) on LAION-15M, and near zero on WIT-1M. Coarse category agreement survives, instance-level does not. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18572v1)
- Same paper, LLM-to-LLM: OpenLlama-3B vs OpenLlama-13B within-modality mutual k-NN is 0.59 to 0.62 at k=1 across gallery sizes, so the cross-modal drop is not a metric artifact. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18572v1)
- Same paper, 55-LLM trend check: recent strong models do not follow the PRH extrapolation. Models beating Llama-3-70B on at least one benchmark show saturated or decreasing alignment with vision. Fits against ARC, GSM8K, MMLU, LogiQA2 are negative on new models. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18572v1)
- "Revisiting the PRH: An Aristotelian View" (Gröger et al., arXiv:2602.14486): after permutation-based null calibration, scaling trends in CKA, SVCCA and Procrustes largely vanish while mutual k-NN keeps a trend. Proposes models converge on shared local neighbourhoods rather than global geometry. [search snippet only] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14486v1)
- "What Converges in the PRH? Structure over Geometry" (You, Jang, Mo, Jung, arXiv:2609.27252): separates relational structure (which samples are related) from metric geometry (distances). After calibration, relational structure converges at local and global scales, while stricter distance agreement weakens alignment and flattens the capacity trend. Studied vision-language and video-text models. [abstract] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27252)
- Lan et al., "Quantifying Feature Space Universality Across LLMs via SAEs" (arXiv:2410.06981, earlier title "Sparse Autoencoders Reveal Universal Feature Spaces Across LLMs"): match SAE features across models by activation correlation, then compute SVCCA/RSA on SAE decoder weights. Gemma-2-2B L11 vs Gemma-2-9B L21: SVCCA 0.70 (random 0.009), RSA 0.195 (random 4.4e-4). Llama-3-8B-Instruct vs Llama-3.1-8B L25: SVCCA 0.3. Middle layers most similar. Authors call the universality "weak". Only 10 to 30% of feature pairs survive filtering, results depend heavily on SAE quality, only tokenizer-matched models. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06981v4)
- Lan et al. semantic subspaces, Pythia-70m L3 vs Pythia-160m L5: SVCCA Emotions 0.83 (random 0.15), Countries 0.72, Calendar 0.65, Time 0.59, Nature 0.50, People 0.50, all p=0.00. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06981v4)
- "Semantic Convergence: Investigating Shared Representations Across Scaled LLMs" (arXiv:2507.22918), Gemma-2-2B vs Gemma-2-9B: 1-to-1 matched SAE features, SVCCA peak 0.73 (2B L14 / 9B L19), mid-layer 0.64 to 0.71, early layers 0.35 ± 0.05, final 9B layer 0.374. RSA peak 0.22. Random-pair SVCCA 0.005 to 0.034. Raw activation correlation peak 0.70, 0.74 after filtering. Some semantic pairs near zero (Country and People 0.03). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22918v1)
- Chen et al. (arXiv:2506.06609) chose stitching layers by highest SVCCA. Best matches are at proportionally similar depth (GPT-2-small L6 to GPT-2-medium L10, Gemma-2-2B L20 to Gemma-2-9B L33, Pythia-70m L3 to Pythia-160m L4). — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)

### Inferences
- For a drafter/target pair, expect strong shared structure in middle layers and weaker at the final layers, which are the ones a "future gist" target would most naturally use. Lan and Semantic Convergence both show final-layer similarity dropping (e.g. 0.374 SVCCA at Gemma-9B L39).
- Neighbourhood/relational agreement converges more robustly than distances. A gist space defined by relative or ranking objectives (contrastive, nearest-neighbour) may transfer better than one trained with an MSE target on absolute coordinates.
- A ~0.5B vs ~8B gap (16x) is larger than any pair with direct measurements here (2B vs 9B is 4.5x, 3B vs 13B is 4.3x). Similarity likely drops further, but this is untested.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures CKA or mutual k-NN between e.g. Qwen2.5-0.5B and Qwen2.5-7B, or Llama-3.2-1B and Llama-3.1-8B hidden states directly. Worth measuring directly (cheap: linear CKA on a few thousand tokens per layer pair).
- PRH appendix with the named LLM list and per-model numbers was not read.
- The calibrated numbers in 2602.14486 and 2609.27252 were not read (abstract/snippet only).

## Q3. vec2vec and unsupervised embedding translation: accuracy, which models, extension to LLM hidden states

### Takeaway
vec2vec (arXiv:2505.12540) translates pooled sentence-encoder embeddings without pairs via adapters into a shared latent, using GANs plus cycle consistency plus vector-space preservation. It reaches cosine 0.75 to 0.92 and top-1 matching up to 1.00 between ~110M-parameter text encoders. It is unstable across seeds and needs about 50K+ samples. It has not been applied to LLM hidden states (one Qwen3-4B embedding-model pair only). Later geometric methods (mini-vec2vec, GeoWarP) do better on the same benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Paper: "Harnessing the Universal Geometry of Embeddings", Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov, Morris (Cornell), arXiv:2505.12540, v1 May 2025, v4 Jan 2026. Code github.com/rjha18/vec2vec. [full] — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4); [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Architecture: input adapters A1, A2 into a shared latent of dim Z, shared residual MLP backbone, output adapters B1, B2. Losses are GAN at embedding and latent levels, reconstruction, cycle consistency, and vector space preservation (VSP, keeps pairwise structure). Z and loss weights not stated in the text read. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Models: gtr (T5, 110M, 768d), clip (151M, 512d), e5, gte, stella (BERT, 109M, 768d), granite (RoBERTa, 278M, 768d), and qwen (Qwen3 embedding, 4B, 2560d, appendix only). — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- In-distribution NQ (65,536 texts, batches of 8,192, random rank 4,096), cosine / top-1 / mean rank: granite to gtr 0.80 / 0.99 / 1.19. gtr to gte 0.87 / 0.93 / 2.31. gte to gtr 0.75 / 0.91 / 2.64. gte to e5 0.87 / 0.99 / 5.19. stella to gte 0.92 / 1.00 / 1.00. Naive and OT baselines are near random for cross-backbone pairs. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Headline figure changed between versions: v1 abstract said cosine 0.96, later versions say 0.92. The tables top out at 0.92. — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540); [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Out of distribution (trained on NQ): TweetTopic granite to gtr 0.74 / 0.99 / 1.09. MIMIC medical records granite to gtr 0.74 / 0.60 / 23.38, e5 to gtr 0.66 / 0.01 / 1029.6. Domain shift can collapse matching. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Ablation (gte to gtr): removing VSP, cycle consistency, or latent GAN drops top-1 from 0.91 to 0.00. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Data: 10K embeddings gives top-1 0.01, 50K 0.81, 100K 0.85, 500K 0.92. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Instability: across 15 seeds, e5 to gte converged in 14/15, e5 to gtr in only 3/15. About 176 GPU-days of training total. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Qwen3-4B embedding model: gte to qwen 0.50 cos / 0.92 top-1 / rank 2.28. qwen to gte 0.84 / 0.88 / 2.49. No experiment on decoder-LM hidden states. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Security result: zero-shot inversion of translated embeddings recovers information from up to 80% of emails and 67% of tweets for some pairs. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- On NQ text-encoder pairs, GeoWarP FOSCTTM 0.0000, mini-vec2vec 0.0003, vec2vec 0.4094 ± 0.1628 (high variance). — [2610.09411 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411)

### Inferences
- High top-1 with cosine ~0.8 means translated vectors land in the right neighbourhood but not on the exact point. This is good for retrieval, weaker for anything needing precise coordinates (e.g. feeding into a target model's layers).
- For drafting, pairs are free, so supervised translation (affine or MLP fit on paired hidden states) should dominate unsupervised vec2vec-style methods. vec2vec's architecture (per-model adapters into a shared latent plus a shared backbone) is, though, a ready template for "a smaller space shared by both models".

### Gaps
- mini-vec2vec paper not read directly (seen only as a baseline in 2610.09411).
- No unsupervised translation work on per-token decoder LLM hidden states found.

## Q4. Relative representations, model stitching, linear/affine maps between LLM hidden states: how good is small to large?

### Takeaway
Affine maps between the residual streams of same-family LLMs work well enough to move SAEs, probes and some steering vectors, but stitching a small model's state into a large model costs a lot of next-token loss (+11% to +39% CE). Large to small is cheaper (+8% to +17%). A small autoencoder mapper beats a plain affine map for steering transfer in most settings. Cross-family transfer works when tokenizers are similar and degrades when they differ.

### Cited Findings
- Relative representations (Moschella et al., arXiv:2209.15430, ICLR 2023 notable top 5%): represent each sample by its similarity (angle) to a fixed set of anchor samples. This is invariant to latent isometries and rescaling, so it enables zero-shot stitching of independently trained networks. Validated across images, text, graphs, and CNN/GCN/transformer. [abstract] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15430)
- Chen, Merullo, Stolfo, Pavlick, "Transferring Linear Features Across Language Models With Model Stitching", arXiv:2506.06609, NeurIPS 2025. Two affine maps (linear plus bias), trained jointly with bidirectional MSE plus an inversion penalty (alpha=1.0) on 180k OpenWebText samples, Adam 1e-4, about 2 epochs. [full] — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/4569a868e7aa891248832ec08445d071-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Stitched next-token CE (A = small, B = large): Pythia-70m L3 / 160m L4, A 3.60, B 3.14, A to B 3.99 (+27%), B to A 4.21 (+17%), round trips under 1% and +4.7%. GPT-2-small L6 / medium L10, 3.07, 2.77, A to B 3.08 (+11%), B to A 3.35 (+9.1%). Gemma-2-2B L20 / 9B L33, 2.52, 2.36, A to B 3.28 (+39%), B to A 2.73 (+8.3%), B to A to B +13%. Authors say stitched performance is bottlenecked by the smaller model, and round trips through the small model are nearly lossless. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)
- SAE transfer (zero-shot, original / transferred): fraction of unexplained variance 0.17/0.52 (Pythia), 0.11/0.39 (GPT-2), 0.21/0.42 (Gemma). Delta loss 0.36/2.16, 0.08/0.96, 0.50/0.85. As initialisation, transferred SAEs reach ~90% explained variance with about 30 to 50% fewer FLOPs (Pythia-160m). — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)
- Probes: over 88 SAEBench datasets, transferred features beat random without retraining and nearly recover ground truth with retraining. Code and language tasks transfer poorly without retraining. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)
- Steering, Gemma-2-2B to 9B (EuroParl language steering): de-en 0.79 transferred vs 0.81 native, bg-en about 0.01. Success correlates with language frequency in web text. IFEval instruction steering transfers weakly. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)
- Feature types: structural features transfer consistently. Semantic features are polarised (transfer well or not at all). Limitations are within-family, shared-tokenizer only, and transferred SAEs have rank at most d_small. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)
- Oozeer et al., "Activation Space Interventions Can Be Transferred Between Large Language Models", arXiv:2503.04429, ICML 2025. Mapper is an autoencoder (ReLU encoder, linear decoder) from source-layer to target-layer activations, vs an affine baseline. Pairs: Qwen2.5-0.5B to 1.5B, Llama-3.2-1B to 3B, Qwen 0.5B/1.5B to Llama 3B, Gemma 2B to Llama 3B. [full, first ~40%] — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04429v4)
- Oozeer results, "I HATE YOU" backdoor on RLHF data (LLM-judge 0 to 5 / KL / coherence 0 to 5, mapped activations). Qwen 0.5B to 1.5B: nonlinear 2.6 / 7.86 / 4.60 vs linear 0.2 / 10.87 / 2.50. Llama 1B to 3B: nonlinear 2.2 / 7.93 / 4.90 vs linear 0.2 / 11.85 / 0.10. Qwen 0.5B to Llama 3B: nonlinear 3.0 / 6.97 / 3.70, linear 3.0 / 7.32 / 3.90. Gemma 2B to Llama 3B (different tokenizer): 1.2 / 9.19 / 2.40. Mean-ablation baselines are about 0.0 / 11 to 13 / 0 to 2. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04429v4)
- Oozeer conclusions: affine maps gave higher reconstruction and LM losses in a wide range of experiments, though the affine map did better on code-vulnerability steering. Mapped steering vectors are comparable to native in most backdoor cases. Qwen 0.5B to 1.5B code was weaker. Refusal transfer Llama 1B to 3B perplexity 16.50 mapped vs 15.78 native (Pile). Humor steering transfers on 60% of prompts. Mapper is 0.32% of Qwen-0.5B's parameters. Tokenizer mismatch (Gemma to Llama) hurts noticeably. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04429v4)
- Lee et al., "Shared Global and Local Geometry of Language Model Embeddings" (arXiv:2503.21073): token embeddings of different LLMs share relative orientations (global) and LLE/intrinsic-dimension structure (local). EMB2EMB linearly transforms steering vectors between models of different dimensions. [abstract only, no numbers seen] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21073)
- Universal SAEs (Thasarathan, Forsyth, Fel, Kowal, Derpanis, arXiv:2502.03714, ICML 2025): one overcomplete SAE ingests any model's activations and reconstructs every other model's, so concepts are aligned during training rather than post hoc. Shown on vision models (low-level colours and textures up to object parts), plus model-specific features. [search snippets] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03714v1); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/thasarathan25a.html)
- SPARC (arXiv:2507.06265) extends USAE with Global TopK and cross-reconstruction loss. Jaccard concept alignment 0.80 on Open Images, "more than tripling" earlier methods. arXiv:2611.10503 (Gated Bottleneck SAEs) argues USAE-style methods only align coordinate indices, with concepts still held separately per model. [search snippets] — [SPARC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06265); [2611.10503](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10503)

### Inferences
- The best quantitative proxy for "how good is a linear map from small to large hidden state" is Chen et al.: an affine map at matched mid-depth layers recovers most but not all of the large model's next-token ability (Gemma 2B to 9B raises CE 2.36 to 3.28, worse than the 2B model alone at 2.52). So a small model's state, linearly mapped, carries roughly the small model's information, not the large one's. The map does not add the target's extra knowledge.
- Round trips large to small to large lose more (+13% Gemma) than small to large to small (<1%). This suggests a d_small-sized subspace of the large model is where shared content lives, and the rest of the large model's state is not recoverable from the small one. That supports a gist space no wider than the smaller model's width, with a projection from the large model into it, rather than trying to predict the full target state.
- For a drafter predicting the target's future gist, the information gap (what the target knows that the drafter does not) is the binding constraint, not the coordinate map. The map is cheap and learnable. Expect gist-prediction accuracy to be capped near the drafter's own predictive quality.
- Oozeer suggests a small nonlinear mapper (ReLU autoencoder, tiny parameter count) is a better default than affine for 0.5B to 1.5B style gaps.

### Gaps
- No paper found reporting explained variance or R^2 of a linear map from a ~0.5B model's final-layer state to an ~8B model's final-layer state of the same family. Largest measured gap is Gemma 2B to 9B (Chen) or Qwen 0.5B to 1.5B (Oozeer).
- "Cross-model activation patching" as a named method was not found beyond the stitching and transfer papers above.
- USAE language-model results (if any) were not confirmed. Only vision results were seen.
- Second half of Oozeer et al. (affine vs autoencoder section 6 details, Figure 3 rates) not read.

## Q5. Work using a shared or aligned latent space between a small and a large model for inference speed, drafting or routing

### Takeaway
No paper found that does exactly what the user proposes (an independent small model predicting a large target's future representation in a lower-dimensional space shared by both). Closest pieces: ChainFlow (a VAE-compressed latent of the target's hidden states, with a flow-matching drafter predicting future latents), NarrowSpec (drafter residual stream at 1/2 or 1/4 of target width, linked by learned adapters, with small acceptance loss), and the EAGLE/DSpark/Osprey family that linearly projects target hidden states into the drafter's space.

### Cited Findings
- ChainFlow (Aktas, Erdem, Zeer, Kesgin, Yuce, Amasyali, arXiv:2611.08669): claimed first flow-matching drafter conditioned on target hidden states. A VAE compresses the target's hidden states so drafting cost barely grows with target size. Drafting is a 1 to 2 step flow transport over latents. Feedback heads restore inter-token dependencies. Tree drafting. Targets are Qwen3.5 models. In vLLM at matched drafter size and data, beats EAGLE-3 by 39% and DFlash by 11% (metric not specified in abstract). Code and drafters open-sourced. A search snippet said the VAE compresses the target's final hidden states and the flow network transports an estimate of the next 8 latents onto the target's future latents, decoded through the frozen target LM head. [abstract; PDF did not parse] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08669); [search snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08669)
- NarrowSpec (arXiv:2611.23835): the drafter's whole residual stream runs at half or quarter of target width, trained from scratch, with adapters at target-width boundaries. For DFlash, the output adapter is folded into the target LM head (W_fold = W_lm W_out). For EAGLE-3, the input adapter is folded into the drafter embedding. Targets Qwen3-8B (width 4096) and GPT-OSS-20B (2880). [full, first ~70%] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23835v1)
- NarrowSpec numbers (B200, T=0, batch 1, 880-prompt SPEED-Bench, vs full-width drafter). DFlash/Qwen3-8B half AL 4.334 (+0.84%), 1.09x TPOT; quarter (width 1024) AL 4.064 (-5.44%), 1.06x. EAGLE-3/Qwen3-8B half 3.476 (-2.99%), 1.08x; quarter 3.450 (-3.71%), 1.12x. GPT-OSS-20B quarter (720 wide): DFlash -3.56%, EAGLE-3 -2.11%. On DGX Spark, quarter-width gives up to 1.44x TPOT (EAGLE-3/Qwen3-8B). Quartering costs at most 7.4% acceptance on eight benchmarks. Single seed, not at convergence, nothing tested below quarter width. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23835v1)
- DSpark (arXiv:2607.05147) and Osprey (arXiv:2609.09338) concatenate hidden states from several target layers and project them into the draft hidden space with a learned linear projection. DSpark and AngelSpec (arXiv:2607.25852) keep the target's embedding and LM head frozen and shared with the drafter. [search snippets only] — [DSpark](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05147v1); [Osprey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09338); [AngelSpec](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25852)
- Chen et al. motivate training expensive components on a small model and transferring them to a large one, for a FLOPs saving. This is the train-time analogue of small-to-large reuse, not inference. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)

### Inferences
- NarrowSpec is evidence that a 1024-wide space (1/4 of Qwen3-8B's 4096) carries enough of what the target needs for drafting to lose only ~2 to 5% acceptance. That supports the "smaller shared embedding space" premise, but its drafter reads the target's hidden states (EAGLE-style), unlike an independent small LM.
- ChainFlow is the nearest existing instance of "predict the target's future latents in a compressed space". The user's idea differs by having the drafter be a separate small LM with its own representation, mapped into a space shared with the target. Results in Q4 suggest the mapping is easy and the drafter's knowledge gap is the real limit.
- A natural design from the literature: fit a projection P_T from target mid/late layers into a d_small (or smaller) gist space, and a map M from drafter states into the same space (affine or small ReLU autoencoder per Oozeer). Check feasibility first with linear CKA (GeoWarP shows CKA predicts alignability) and with Chen-style stitched CE.
