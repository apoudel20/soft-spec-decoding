Items marked "(ID recalled, not re-fetched)" are well-known papers whose arXiv IDs come from prior knowledge and were not opened this session. ## Q1. Hierarchical and latent-plan generation (what they predict, how the latent is trained, results)

### Takeaway
A large body of work trains LMs to predict a compressed representation of the future (sentence embeddings, autoencoder latents, SAE concepts, JEPA embeddings, reverse-LM "future summaries"). All of it targets better modeling or planning quality, not inference speed. The closest training-side precedent for the "target emits a long-horizon plan" half of the idea is Future Summary Prediction (FSP, arXiv 2510.14751), which trains an auxiliary head on the target to regress a learned summary of the long-term future.

### Cited Findings
- **Future Summary Prediction (FSP), "Beyond Multi-Token Prediction: Pretraining LLMs with Future Summaries", Mahajan, Ahuja et al., FAIR/Meta + Mila + CMU, ICLR 2026, arXiv 2510.14751 (verified via search snippets).** An auxiliary head predicts "a compact representation of the long-term future". Two targets are used. One is hand-crafted (bag-of-words of a future window). The other is learned, where a reverse LM's hidden state over the future is the target and the forward model's head matches it with an l2 loss. Experiments at 3B and 8B report gains "of up to 5%" on math and coding benchmarks ([arXiv 2510.14751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14751), [ICLR 2026 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008683)).
- **Future Token Prediction (FTP), arXiv 2410.18160 (Oct 2024).** An encoder produces a per-position embedding from which a small decoder predicts the next N tokens. Top-layer vectors "vary smoothly along a text sequence" by adjacent-token cosine similarity ([arXiv 2410.18160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160)).
- **Large Concept Models (LCM), Meta FAIR, Dec 2024, arXiv 2412.08821 (verified).** A "concept" is a sentence embedded in SONAR space (200 languages, text and speech). The model does autoregressive next-sentence-embedding prediction. Variants include MSE regression, several diffusion variants, and quantized SONAR. The authors claim strong zero-shot multilingual generalization versus same-size LLMs ([arXiv 2412.08821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08821), [Meta page](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/large-concept-models-language-modeling-in-a-sentence-representation-space/)).
- **PLANNER, Zhang, Gu, Wu, Zhai, Susskind, Jaitly (Apple), NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2306.02531 (verified).** A latent diffusion planning module generates "semantic paragraph embeddings" coarse-to-fine, and an autoregressive decoder renders text. Evaluated on semantic generation, completion, summarization. The abstract gives no numbers ([arXiv 2306.02531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02531)).
- **Semformer, Yin, Ding, Song, Zhang, EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2409.11143 (verified).** Planning tokens appended after the prefix are trained to "predict the latent semantic representations of the response", with targets from an autoencoder used only at training time. "Near-perfect performance" on graph path-finding with reduced shortcut learning. A 125M model pretrained from scratch is evaluated on perplexity, ICL and summarization (abstract has no numbers) ([arXiv 2409.11143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11143), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1039.pdf)).
- **CoCoMix, "LLM Pretraining with Continuous Concepts", Tack, Lanchantin, Yu, ... Weston, Li (Meta), ICLR 2026, arXiv 2502.08524 (verified).** The model predicts continuous concepts from a pretrained sparse autoencoder and interleaves them with token hidden states. It is reported as more sample efficient than next-token prediction, knowledge distillation and pause tokens. The abstract says joint concept learning plus interleaving is "critical" ([arXiv 2502.08524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08524), [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/59056767478c7df64e6250eadfeb0a04-Abstract-Conference.html)).
- **LLM-JEPA, Hai Huang (Atlassian), Yann LeCun (NYU), Randall Balestriero (Brown), ICLR 2026, arXiv 2509.14252 (verified via search snippets).** The loss is the LM loss plus lambda times a JEPA term that predicts the embedding of a paired view (for example text to code). It reports outperforming standard training across Llama3, OpenELM, Gemma2, Olmo on NL-RX, GSM8K, Spider, RottenTomatoes, and resisting overfitting under LoRA fine-tuning. Code at github.com/rbalestr-lab/llm-jepa ([arXiv 2509.14252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14252)). Follow-up "Semantic Tube Prediction" by the same authors, arXiv 2602.22617 ([arXiv 2602.22617](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22617)).
- **Next Concept Prediction, arXiv 2602.08984 (2026, snippet only).** It combines concept prediction with next-token prediction, is JEPA-inspired, and claims implicit planning before generation ([arXiv 2602.08984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08984v1)).
- **Next-Latent Prediction Transformers Learn Compact World Models, arXiv 2511.05963 (title only, surfaced by search, not read)** ([arXiv 2511.05963](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.05963)).
- **Block Diffusion (BD3-LM), Arriola, Gokaslan, ... Kuleshov, ICLR 2025 Oral, arXiv 2503.09573 (verified).** It interpolates between discrete denoising diffusion and AR. It supports arbitrary-length generation, KV caching and parallel token sampling, and claims "new state-of-the-art performance among diffusion models on language modeling benchmarks" (abstract has no numbers) ([arXiv 2503.09573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09573)).
- Related, ID recalled, not re-fetched: planning tokens, "Guiding Language Model Reasoning with Planning Tokens", Wang et al. 2023 ([arXiv 2310.05707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05707)). LD4LG, "Latent Diffusion for Language Generation", Lovelace et al. ([arXiv 2212.09462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09462)). Pause tokens, Goyal et al. ([arXiv 2310.02226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)). Coconut, continuous latent reasoning, Hao et al. ([arXiv 2412.06769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769)). Multi-token prediction, Gloeckle et al. ([arXiv 2404.19737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)).

### Inferences
- The training recipes the idea proposes for the plan (VAE-style autoencoder target as in Semformer/PLANNER, JEPA target as in LLM-JEPA, reverse-LM summary as in FSP) all exist. None of these papers measures drafter acceptance or uses the latent at inference for acceleration. So "how to train a long-horizon latent on the target" is prior art, and "what that latent is worth to a drafter" is open.
- FSP is the cleanest off-the-shelf target for the plan head. Its learned-summary variant already has the "summary of the next many tokens computed from hidden states" shape the idea needs.
- LCM and PLANNER produce sentence or paragraph latents that drive generation directly (lossy by construction relative to any token-level target). They are useful as architecture inspiration for the coarse plan head, not as lossless precedents.

### Gaps
- Exact numbers for PLANNER, BD3-LM, CoCoMix and Semformer are not in their abstracts. The full papers were not read.
- "Beyond next token prediction" hierarchical models, Cosmos-style latent diffusion for language, and 2025 latent-reasoning diffusion papers were not searched individually. No 2025 to 2026 latent-CoT survey was read.

## Q2. Semantic and step-level speculation (which are lossless)

### Takeaway
Almost every step-level or semantic speculation method is lossy. It accepts drafts that are judged semantically equivalent or high reward rather than token-identical. None uses a latent plan produced by the target to condition token drafting.

### Cited Findings
- **SpecReason, Pan et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2504.07891 (verified via search).** A small reasoning model drafts steps, and the large model judges and falls back. 1.4 to 3.0x speedup over vanilla LRM inference with accuracy +0.4 to 9.0%. Stacking on token-level SD gives a further 8.8 to 58.0% latency reduction. **Lossy** (semantic equivalence threshold) ([arXiv 2504.07891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07891v2), [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/126642)).
- **Speculative Thinking, Yang, Yue, Chaudhary, Han, arXiv 2504.12329 (verified via search).** Training-free. A small model hands hard segments to a large mentor. With the 32B model's help, 1.5B accuracy on MATH500 goes from 83.2% to 89.4%. **Lossy** (output is not the large model's distribution) ([arXiv 2504.12329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12329v1)).
- **Lookahead Reasoning, Fu, Ge, Shao, Deng, Zhang, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2506.19830 (verified via search).** Step-level parallelism on top of token SD. The draft proposes future steps and the target verifies them semantically. It raises SD speedup "from 1.4x to 2.1x" while preserving answer quality. **Lossy at step level**, though inner token SD stays exact ([arXiv 2506.19830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19830v1)).
- **Reward-Guided Speculative Decoding (RSD), Liao, Xu, Dong, Li, Monz, Savarese, Sahoo, Xiong (Salesforce), arXiv 2501.19324 (verified).** A PRM scores draft steps and a threshold mixture decides when to call the target. Up to 4.4x fewer FLOPs than target-only and up to +3.5 average accuracy over parallel decoding. **Explicitly biased**, adding "a controlled bias to prioritize high-reward outputs" ([arXiv 2501.19324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19324)).
- **SemanticSpec, "Beyond Tokens: Semantic-Aware Speculative Decoding ... by Probing Internal States", Dong, Wang, Lin, Chen, Hassan, arXiv 2602.03708, Findings of EMNLP 2026 (verified via search).** It verifies whole semantic sequences using a 3-layer MLP probe on target hidden states that estimates the probability of a meaning. Reported 1.67x on DeepSeek-R1-32B and 2.66x on QwQ-32B over token-level SD (a third-party review says "up to 2.7x"). **Lossy** (semantic acceptance) ([arXiv 2602.03708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03708), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1239.pdf)). Note this is a probe on target internals, but it is used for verification, not for conditioning a drafter.
- **Speculative CoT, arXiv 2504.19095, and Speculative Search, arXiv 2505.02865 (titles surfaced, not read)** ([2504.19095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19095), [2505.02865](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02865)).
- **Skeleton-of-Thought, Ning et al., arXiv 2307.15337 (ID recalled, not re-fetched).** It prompts for an outline and then expands points in parallel. Lossy and prompting-based ([arXiv 2307.15337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15337)).
- **Lossless "hierarchical" SD papers are about verification or draft sources, not plans.** Hierarchical Speculative Decoding (HSD), Zhou et al., arXiv 2601.05724, is a provably lossless verification scheme reporting "over 12%" gain when integrated into EAGLE-3 ([arXiv 2601.05724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05724)). HiSpec, arXiv 2510.01336, uses early-exit intermediate verifiers ([arXiv 2510.01336](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01336)). Hierarchical Drafting, arXiv 2502.05609 (NAACL Findings 2025), is a database drafting method with temporal-locality hierarchy ([arXiv 2502.05609](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05609)).

### Inferences
- The proposed idea sits in a different cell from all step-level work. Those methods use the hierarchy to relax acceptance (lossy). The idea uses the hierarchy only to improve drafts, while keeping token-exact verification. This contrast is a clean framing point for a paper.
- The name "hierarchical speculative decoding" is taken (arXiv 2601.05724 and HiSpec) for unrelated meanings. A new paper should pick a different name.

### Gaps
- Lossy-SD surveys (for example arXiv 2607.26627, "Revisiting Lossy Verification", and 2607.08690) surfaced but were not read.

## Q3. Drafters conditioned on target internals (any with a long-horizon or compressed future summary?)

### Takeaway
Conditioning drafters on target hidden states is standard (Medusa, EAGLE 1/2/3, GLIDE, HASS, Falcon, DFlash). Two papers go further and pass a target-derived *future-oriented* vector to the drafter. **ConFu (arXiv 2603.08899)** is the closest prior art found. The target, frozen but augmented with soft prompts and a "contemplate token", emits a future-prediction vector that conditions an EAGLE-3 drafter, losslessly. **SD² (arXiv 2511.09844)** feeds a once-per-cycle steering vector from multi-layer verifier states, trained to carry information about the next k tokens. Both are short-horizon (about one draft block) and learned only through the drafter's KL loss. Neither uses an explicit long-horizon representation target or a two-level plan/token drafter.

### Cited Findings
- **ConFu: "Contemplate the Future for Better Speculative Sampling", Qin (UCLA), Goel, Gagrani, Garrepalli, Lee (Qualcomm AI Research), Sun (UCLA), arXiv 2603.08899 v3 (21 Apr 2026). Venue not stated on page ("ICML" appears only as a keyword) (verified, HTML read).**
  - Mechanism. Learnable soft-prompt tokens are prepended to the target's KV cache. A contemplate token is appended to the prefix, and its last-layer hidden state is the "future" vector f. The attention mask lets only contemplate tokens attend to the soft prompts, so ordinary prefix representations are unchanged. **The target weights are frozen. No LoRA** ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - Dynamic contemplate tokens use a MoE router over learnable expert embeddings, conditioned on the last accepted token's hidden state. After verification, the future vector of the last accepted token goes to the drafter in the next iteration ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - Training. KL between target and draft next-token distributions under train-time testing. "No additional auxiliary losses are required". The future vector is learned only through the drafter loss. Anchor-token sampling and replication share one vector across a window of nearby tokens. Drafter rollout length L is about 7 (ablations 4 and 10). **No explicit horizon or representation target is defined** ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - Drafter. EAGLE-3 single-layer transformer with f as an extra input, initialized from EAGLE-3 checkpoints ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - Lossless. It claims it "does not alter the sampling distribution of the target model" ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - Numbers (SpecBench, BS=1, one H100, T=0, 30 nodes). Llama-3.2-3B gets tau 4.41 / SR 2.11 vs EAGLE-3 4.00 / 1.83. Llama-3.1-8B gets tau 5.01 / SR 2.69 vs EAGLE-3 4.59 / 2.36. Qwen3-4B gets about +21.0% tau and +19.8% SR. The abstract says 8 to 11% on Llama-3 3B/8B and about 20% on Qwen3-4B. Removing MoE drops tau 5.01 to 4.97, and removing MoE plus replication drops it to 4.81 ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - Stated cost. Verification processes 2T tokens for T draft nodes (one contemplate token per node). The paper notes that SD "does not permit fine-tuning the target model, as doing so would alter model behavior", which is why it uses soft prompts ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - It cites BiTA (soft prompts for future-token decoding) and pause tokens as bases ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
- **SD², "Steering Pretrained Drafters during Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2511.09844 (verified, HTML read).** The steering vector g_t = W[h_t, m_t, l_t] uses verifier hidden states from layers 3, L/2 and L-2 at the last returned (first rejected) token, plus a layer norm. It is added as a bias to the up-projection of every drafter MLP layer once per drafting stage. The verifier is frozen and the method is lossless. Training uses a random offset delta in [1, k] that pairs g_{t-delta} with x_t, so the vector must encode the next k tokens. Loss is KL(pi_V || pi_D). Pairs tested are Vicuna-1.3-13B/Llama-160M, Qwen3-8B and 14B/Qwen3-0.6B, and Llama-3.1-8B/Llama-3.2-1B. Results are "up to 35%" more accepted tokens under sampling and 22% under greedy (the paper is inconsistent on whether 22% is throughput), with average speedup +16.3% at T=1 and +19.5% at T=0. On Vicuna at T=1, block efficiency goes from 1.88 to 2.96 ([arXiv 2511.09844](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09844)).
- **DFlash, Chen, Liang, Liu, ICML 2026, arXiv 2602.06036 (verified via search).** A block-diffusion drafter drafts a block in one forward pass. Hidden states from a fixed set of shallow-to-deep target layers are concatenated, projected, and injected into every draft layer's KV cache. It reports "over 6x lossless acceleration" and up to 2.5x higher speedup than EAGLE-3 ([arXiv 2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06036v1)). Follow-ups are DFlare (layer-wise fusion, arXiv 2606.02091) and DFlow (verifier information flow, arXiv 2609.06498) ([2606.02091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02091), [2609.06498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06498)).
- **"Your LLM Knows the Future", Samragh et al. (Apple), arXiv 2507.11851 (verified).** Uses mask-style inputs to predict multiple future tokens jointly, with "a gated LoRA formulation that preserves the original LLM's functionality", a learnable sampler module, and quadratic speculative expansion. Claims nearly 5x speedup on code and math and almost 2.5x on chat and knowledge "without any loss in quality" ([arXiv 2507.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11851)). This is direct precedent for the "gated LoRA on the target that leaves its token outputs unchanged" part of the idea.
- **DBLast, Karimi, Gao, Hassanpour, arXiv 2608.05448 (Aug 2026, verified abstract).** A dependent block drafter with "a low-rank latent mixture over token positions". The latent is internal to the drafter, not produced by the target. It improves accepted length over independent block sampling, especially at high entropy, on Qwen3-4B/8B ([arXiv 2608.05448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05448)).
- **Variational Speculative Decoding, arXiv 2602.05774 (snippet only).** It treats draft training as variational inference over latent draft paths ([arXiv 2602.05774](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05774v4)). **ChainFlow, arXiv 2611.08669 (snippet only).** Few-step latent flow matching over target hidden states ([arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08669)).
- **Recurrent Drafter (ReDrafter), arXiv 2403.09919.** It keeps the target frozen to preserve its distribution and lists joint target/drafter training as future work ([arXiv 2403.09919](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09919v1)).
- **Speculative Streaming (EMNLP 2025).** It folds multi-stream n-gram speculation into the target itself, with no separate drafter ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.986.pdf)).
- SD² summarizes the conditioning landscape. EAGLE 1/2/3 use verifier hidden states. Medusa uses the last hidden state. Hydra uses drafted-token embeddings. Mixture of Attentions and GliDe use verifier KV. HASS-style methods address the train/inference gap ([arXiv 2511.09844](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09844)).
- ID recalled, not re-fetched: EAGLE ([2401.15077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15077)), EAGLE-2 ([2406.16858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16858)), EAGLE-3 ([2503.01840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01840)), GliDe with a CaPE ([2402.02082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02082)), Clover ([2405.00263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00263)), Falcon ([2412.12639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12639)), HASS ([2408.15766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15766)), Medusa ([2401.10774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774)), Hydra ([2402.05109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05109)), BiTA ([2401.12522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12522)), Speculative Streaming ([2402.11131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11131)).

### Inferences
- ConFu has already shown, losslessly, that a target-produced future vector (via a frozen-weight side path) helps a drafter by about 8 to 21% tau. Any "symbiotic target emits a plan for the drafter" claim must be positioned against ConFu, not claimed as new.
- ConFu's future vector is short-horizon. It replicates across a small window, trains only through about 7-step draft KL, and has no auxiliary target. SD²'s vector is explicitly k-step. Neither targets 32 tokens to a whole response, and neither trains the plan with a VAE, JEPA or reverse-LM objective. A long-horizon plan with an explicit representation loss (FSP-style) is the main surviving technical difference.
- ConFu's side path costs 2T verification tokens. The idea's "side heads" variant (a plan head reading existing hidden states, no extra tokens) could avoid that cost. This is a concrete axis to beat ConFu on.
- On the gated-LoRA variant, the Apple paper (2507.11851) already shows gated LoRA can add predictive capacity while preserving outputs. ConFu shows the attention-mask isolation trick. Losslessness relative to the *original* target holds only if the gate or mask guarantees that the token-path logits are bit-identical. That needs to be stated and tested (for example kernel nondeterminism and shared KV).

### Gaps
- ConFu's venue (ICML 2026?) is unconfirmed. Its exact window length l is not given in the page text.
- Falcon, Clover-2, GLIDE and HASS were not re-read, so it is unverified whether any of them adds a multi-token future summary. From memory they condition on per-token features or KV only.
- arXiv 2609.36173 ("Adjacent Causal Injection"), 2605.29707 (Domino), 2608.02123 (parent-conditioned drafting), 2609.09338 (Osprey) and 2611.06425 (D3D) surfaced but were not read. Any of them could contain a block-level summary signal.

## Q5. Verdict: has anyone used a target-produced long-horizon latent plan to condition a lossless drafter?

### Takeaway
Partially yes. ConFu (arXiv 2603.08899) already conditions a lossless EAGLE-3 drafter on a target-produced future vector, with a frozen target plus soft prompts and a contemplate token. The *long-horizon* (32 tokens to whole response), *explicitly trained* (VAE, JEPA or FSP target) plan feeding a *two-level coarse-plan/fine-token drafter* was not found. That combination appears novel, but only as a delta on ConFu, SD² and FSP.

### Cited Findings
- **Already done.**
  - Target-produced future vector conditioning a lossless drafter ([ConFu, arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)).
  - Verifier multi-layer vector trained to encode the next k tokens, steering the drafter once per cycle ([SD², arXiv 2511.09844](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09844)).
  - Multi-layer target features as drafter context ([DFlash, arXiv 2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06036v1)).
  - Gated LoRA on the target that preserves original outputs while adding future prediction ([arXiv 2507.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11851)).
  - Training a target-side head to predict a learned long-horizon future summary ([FSP, arXiv 2510.14751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14751)).
  - Training planning tokens toward autoencoder latents of the response ([Semformer, arXiv 2409.11143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11143)).
  - JEPA objectives for LLMs ([LLM-JEPA, arXiv 2509.14252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14252)).
  - Step-level hierarchical speculation (all lossy) ([SpecReason](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07891v2), [Lookahead Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19830v1), [RSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19324)).
- ConFu has no defined horizon and trains f only via about 7-step draft KL with "no additional auxiliary losses" ([arXiv 2603.08899](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08899)). SD²'s horizon is k draft tokens ([arXiv 2511.09844](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09844)).

### Inferences
- **Novelty claims that likely survive.**
  1. Plan horizon far beyond the draft block (32 tokens up to the full response), with the plan refreshed or re-encoded as tokens are accepted.
  2. An explicit representation-learning objective for the plan (reverse-LM or FSP summary, VAE posterior, or JEPA target), combined with a drafter-acceptance loss, plus an ablation showing it beats ConFu's KL-only learned vector.
  3. A two-level drafter (coarse plan head that advances or refines the plan, plus fine token head) as opposed to ConFu's single EAGLE-3 layer with one extra input.
  4. A zero-extra-token side-head design that avoids ConFu's 2T verification overhead.
  5. Evidence that long-horizon plans help most where per-token drafters fail (long reasoning or code, higher temperature, deep trees or large blocks such as DFlash-style block drafters).
- **Claims that do not survive as stated.** "First to have the target emit a latent for the drafter" (ConFu, and partly SD²). "First lossless SD with target internals" (EAGLE and others). "Hierarchical speculative decoding" as a name (taken by 2601.05724). "Gated LoRA preserving the target" as a mechanism (2507.11851). "Symbiotic" is new framing, but ConFu is already a target-side augmentation trained for the drafter.
- **Three closest papers to cite.** (1) ConFu, arXiv 2603.08899, the closest. (2) SD², arXiv 2511.09844, a future-encoding verifier vector steering the drafter. (3) Future Summary Prediction, arXiv 2510.14751, the long-horizon learned summary on the target, not used for SD. Honorable mentions are DFlash (2602.06036) as the drafter-architecture baseline, Apple gated LoRA MTP (2507.11851), and Semformer (2409.11143) or LLM-JEPA (2509.14252) for the plan objective.
